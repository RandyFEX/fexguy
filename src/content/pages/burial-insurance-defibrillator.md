If your device was placed recently, many carriers will only offer guaranteed issue whole life insurance with a waiting period. Over time, if your condition stabilizes, you may qualify for better burial insurance options with full coverage. The key is to apply to the right company based on your exact timeline.</p>

<h2 id="best-option"><strong><strong>What Is My Best Insurance Option If I Have A Defibrillator?</strong></strong></h2>

<p>How long your defibrillator has been hanging out in your chest is a big deal when it comes to your insurance options.<br><br><strong>DEFIBRILLATOR IMPLANTED OVER 2 YEARS AGO</strong></p>

<p>An implantable cardioverter defibrillator (ICD) isn’t just a mouthful – it’s your lifeline. These gadgets are implanted in your chest or abdomen to save you from sudden cardiac arrest. Yep, it’s a big deal and counts as circulatory surgery.</p>

<p>If your ICD has been in place for over 24 months, congrats! You can get a level death benefit plan with first-day coverage from a bunch of burial insurance companies.</p>

<p>You’ll get rates similar to those of folks in perfect health and be fully protected from day one. Plus, you qualify for the lowest premiums.</p>

<p><br><strong>DEFIBRILLATOR IMPLANTED WITHIN 24 MONTHS</strong></p>

<p>Got your ICD within the last two years? No worries! Our preferred company will simply ask, “Been hospitalized twice in the last two years?” If you can say “Nope,” you’re on the fast track to a first-day coverage plan, as long as you’re in the right zip code.</p>

<p>But if you’re still in the hospital or you’ve had two or more visits in the past couple of years, then it’s time to go for guaranteed-issue whole life insurance. Sure, there’s a two-year waiting period, but that’s way better than kicking yourself later for waiting too long to get covered.</p>

<p><br><strong>DEFIBRILLATOR BATTERY CHANGES</strong></p>

<p>Switching out a defibrillator battery? That’s just a quick outpatient tune-up.</p>

<p>Some insurance companies might treat this like major heart surgery, meaning you’d face a waiting period and higher premiums if it’s fresh.</p>

<p>But here’s the kicker: we know burial insurance companies that don’t fuss over battery changes. If you’ve just had one, we can connect you with a company that offers first-day coverage without the wait.</p>

<h2 id="do-i-need-medical-exam"><br><strong>Do I Need A Medical Exam To Qualify For Burial Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>Absolutely not! No medical exams required for getting burial insurance with a defibrillator.</p>

<p>You’ll just need to answer a few straightforward questions about your health. It’s smooth sailing from there – no medical records, no blood, no urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the green light from the insurance company, often in a matter of minutes!</p>

<h2 id="underwriting"><br><strong>Burial Insurance Underwriting If You Have A Defibrillator</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Burial insurance companies have two ways of digging into your health:</strong></p>

<p><strong>FIRST</strong> – They’ll hit you with a bunch of health questions. Your answers will make or break your eligibility.</p>

<p><strong>SECOND</strong> – They’ll snoop through your prescription history electronically to see what’s really going on with your health.</p>

<p>A defibrillator implant is considered a <a href="/burial-insurance-circulatory-surgery/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">circulatory surgery</a>. Most burial insurance companies are nosy about recent circulatory surgeries, usually within the last 24 months. It’s rare for them to care about surgeries beyond two years ago.</p>

<p><strong>HEATH QUESTIONS:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Prior to age 50 or during the past 24 months, have you been hospitalized for heart or circulatory surgery (including pacemaker, heart valve replacement, bypass, angioplasty, stent implant, or any procedure to improve circulation to the heart or brain?</li>
<li>Within the past 24 months, have you been treated or hospitalized for heart or circulatory surgery (including pacemaker, bypass, heart valve replacement, angioplasty, stent implant, or any procedure to improve circulation to the heart or brain?</li>
<li>Within the past 2 years, have you had or been diagnosed with angina, heart attack, cardiomyopathy, or any type of heart or circulatory surgery?</li>
</ul>

<p>In a nutshell, qualifying for burial or final expense insurance hinges on how long it’s been since your last surgery. If your defibrillator implant is fresh, it might jack up your premium and stick you with a waiting period.</p>

<p id="WHAT-MEDICATIONS-DO-THE-INSURANCE-COMPANIES-LOOK-FOR-WHEN-I-HAVE-LUPUS"><br><strong>PRESCRIPTION HISTORY CHECK</strong></p>

<p>Insurance companies will also peek into your prescription history during their underwriting process.</p>

<p>If you’re popping pills for other heart issues related to your defibrillator, it could mess with the plans and rates you qualify for.</p>

<h2 id="faq"><strong>   Frequently Asked Questions </strong></h2>

<p><br><strong>What heart conditions require a defibrillator?</strong></p>

<p>A defibrillator is your heart’s best friend when it stops beating, a condition known as cardiac arrest. But it’s not just for that – it also zaps other pesky heart issues like ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation back into line.</p>

<p><br><strong>Is a defibrillator implant considered surgery in life insurance?</strong></p>

<p>You bet it is! A defibrillator implant counts as surgery in the eyes of life insurance companies.</p>

<p><br><strong>Do I need to tell insurance about my defibrillator use?</strong></p>

<p>Absolutely! Spill the beans about your defibrillator use to your insurance company. Keeping it a secret could lead to denied claims or jacked-up premiums down the road.</p>

<p><br><strong>Can I get insurance if I have a defibrillator and use blood thinners?</strong></p>

<p>Yes, you can still get life insurance even if you’ve got a defibrillator and are on blood thinners. Many insurers see a defibrillator as a minor bump in the road and won’t charge you extra for it.</p>

<p><br><strong>Can you get first-day coverage insurance if you have a defibrillator?</strong></p>

<p>Yes, you can land first-day coverage insurance with a defibrillator. Just be ready for your insurer to ask why you need that handy device.</p>

<p><br><strong>What are the things that may affect my eligibility if I have a defibrillator?</strong></p>

<p>Several factors might mess with your life insurance eligibility if you have a defibrillator. These include a recent heart attack or stroke, using blood thinners, and how long it’s been since your defibrillator was implanted.</p>
