<h3 id="Week-1-–-Organ-And-Tissue-Donation"><strong>STEP 1 – ORGAN AND TISSUE DONATION</strong></h3>

<p>Thinking about organ, tissue, or whole-body donation should be part of your funeral planning. Donation is a great way to help others and advance science. Whole-body donation is an excellent way to avoid the cost of funerals and burial.</p>

<p>If you choose to donate organs or tissues, it is best to do it during your driver’s license renewal. Check the box for “organ donor” for whole body donation. Check the medical schools or independent donation programs near your area.</p>

<figure><img decoding="async" loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Week-1-Organ-and-Tissue-Donation-1024x536.png" alt=""></figure>

<p>Whole-body donation programs can provide free copies of death certificates, body transportation, cremation, and the return of your ashes to your family.</p>

<p>Let your family know if you choose to donate your organ, tissue, or body to science. Also, make sure that you leave some instructions in your will so that your loved ones will know what to do when you pass away.</p>

<p>Make a backup plan in case the program declines to accept your body donation. The best way to do it is to purchase a burial insurance policy to cover your end-of-life expenses. This way, you will not be a burden to your family when you pass away.</p>

<h3 id="Week-2-–-Choosing-Your-Method-Of-Disposition"><strong><strong>STEP </strong>2 – CHOOSING YOUR METHOD OF DISPOSITION</strong></h3>

<p>Determine if you want to be buried or cremated. Choosing the best option should be made before you pass away so that your loved ones will know your final wishes.</p>

<p>Choosing between burial and cremation is a personal prerogative. It should be based on your budget, personal beliefs, religion, and other preferences.</p>

<p>We enumerated below the pros and cons of burial and cremation to help you decide on what method of disposition to use.</p>

<figure><img decoding="async" loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Week-2-Method-of-Disposition-1024x536.png" alt=""></figure>

<p><strong>Burial Pros:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Burial is traditional and more conventional.</li>
<li>Most religions around the world widely accept burial.</li>
<li>Burial provides family and survivors a permanent gravesite to visit.</li>
<li>Burial allows you to be interred close to your loved ones.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Burial Cons:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Burial is more expensive than cremation. The average cost of a funeral is around $9,135.</li>
<li>Visiting a gravesite may be difficult for family members who live far away.</li>
<li>Burials have a negative effect on the environment.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Cremation Pros:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Cremation is more affordable than burial.</li>
<li>Cremation helps save ground space.</li>
<li>Urns are portable, and family members can take them if they move.</li>
<li>Cremation offers flexibility in memorialization.</li>
<li>Cremation is more environmentally friendly than burial.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Cremation Cons:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Not all religions accept the practice of cremation.</li>
<li>Cremation is permanent and non-reversible. Remains cannot be exhumed later.</li>
<li>Cremation is not widely available.</li>
</ul>

<h3 id="Week-5-–-Writing-An-Obituary"><strong><strong>STEP </strong>5 – WRITING AN OBITUARY</strong></h3>

<p>Before you draft your obituary, check your local newspaper and note the format to know what to include. Obituaries are no longer restricted to newspapers. It can also be published on memorial sites and social media.</p>

<p><strong>Different Parts of an Obituary:</strong></p>

<p>1. Announcement of death</p>

<p>2. Summary of your life</p>

<p>3. Family information</p>

<p>4. Funeral or memorial service information</p>

<p>5. Special messages</p>

<p>6. Photo</p>

<figure><img decoding="async" loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Week-5-Writing-an-Obituary-1024x536.png" alt=""></figure>

<p><br><strong>Tips in Writing an Obituary:</strong></p>

<p>1. Prepare the basic details</p>

<ul>
<li>Full name</li>
<li>Date of birth and age</li>
<li>City of residence</li>
<li>Spouse or partner’s name</li>
<li>Children’s name</li>
<li>School attended</li>
<li>Years in the military service</li>
<li>Honors and awards received</li>
<li>Organization affiliation</li>
<li>Religious denomination and address</li>
<li>Funeral home and address</li>
<li>Days of visitation and time</li>
<li>Funeral service date, time, and address</li>
<li>Memorial service date, time, and address</li>
<li>Interment date, time, and address</li>
</ul>

<p>2<strong>. Things you should never mention in an obituary</strong></p>

<p>Some things should never go into an obituary because this information can make you and your family vulnerable to identity theft or fraud:</p>

<ul>
<li>Complete date of birth</li>
<li>Complete home address</li>
<li>Mother’s maiden name</li>
</ul>

<p>The reason for not including this information is that identity thieves can be a real problem. Some unscrupulous people may try to use your loved one’s identity to access bank accounts and personal credit.</p>


<h3 id="Week-6-–-Choosing-A-Cemetery-Or-Burial-Plot"><strong><strong>STEP </strong>6 – CHOOSING A CEMETERY OR BURIAL PLOT</strong></h3>

<p>Reserving a burial plot or buying a plot in advance can be cheaper than buying a plot at the last minute.</p>

<p>A burial plot is an asset, and it can appreciate in value with time.&#160;</p>

<figure><img decoding="async" loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Week-6-Choosing-a-Cemetery-or-Burial-Plot-1024x536.png" alt=""></figure>

<p><strong>Preventive Measures When Buying A Cemetery or Burial Plots</strong></p>

<p>1. Before buying a burial plot, make sure that the seller is the owner on the record with the cemetery. Suppose the seller inherited the burial plot and the original owner neglected to inform the cemetery. In that case, they won’t be able to transfer the ownership until the inheritance issue is ironed out.</p>

<p>2. Don’t forget to ask what’s included in the price. Some sellers include the headstone in the price.</p>

<p>3. Check what you are paying for. Some sellers only sell the rights to the plot, and you need to pay for an in-ground crypt which may incur additional costs and fees.</p>

<h3 id="Week-10-–-Preventing-Identity-Theft"><strong><strong>STEP </strong>10 </strong>– <strong>PREVENTING IDENTITY THEFT</strong></h3>

<p>Preventing identity theft after your death must also be included in your funeral planning guide.</p>

<p><strong>Identity theft may attempt to steal the following:</strong></p>

<p>1. Your social security number</p>

<p>2. Your government benefits</p>

<p>3. Your tax returns</p>

<p>4. Your driver’s license</p>

<p>5. Your medical information</p>

<h3 id="Week-11-–-Proper-Medication-Disposal"><strong><strong>STEP </strong>11 – PROPER MEDICATION DISPOSAL</strong></h3>

<p>Remind your family to dispose of your medication after you are gone. This is necessary if there are small children in the home. Exposure to dangerous medicine is a major cause of unintentional poisoning in children.</p>

<p><strong>Things you should not do with unused medicines:</strong></p>

<p>1. Don’t put unused medicines in the recycle bin,</p>

<p>2. Don’t put medicines in the trash without dissolving them.</p>

<p>3. Don’t give unused medicines to anyone else even if they have the same ailments.</p>

<p>4. Don’t flush the medication down the toilet.</p>

<figure><img decoding="async" loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Week-11-Proper-Medication-Disposal-1024x536.png" alt=""></figure>

<p><strong>Proper medication disposal:</strong></p>

<p>1. Take-back programs – drop the unused medications at the take-back site. Authorized take-back centers may include hospitals, pharmacies, and local law enforcement agencies.</p>

<p>2. Donation – if the unused medication is unexpired, sealed, and not a narcotic or opioid, you can donate them to some authorized collectors like hospitals, non-profit clinics, law enforcement collection sites, pharmacies, or prescription drug collection programs.</p>

<p>3. Flushing – some medications are labeled good for flushing down the sink or toilet. Check the <a href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/disposal-unused-medicines-what-you-should-know/drug-disposal-flush-potentially-dangerous-medicine#FlushList" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">FDA flush list</a> if the unused medication can be flushed. </p>

<p>4. Dispose of in the trash – follow these steps for proper trash disposal</p>

<ul>
<li>Do not crush the pills. Keep the medications in its original container to help identify the contents if they are accidentally ingested.</li>
<li>Remove or mark out your personal information to make it unreadable, but leave the name and dose of the medication on the container. Blacken your name and prescription number for safety. This will protect your identity and the privacy of your health information.</li>
<li>Add some inedible substance to the medication and then replace the lid.</li>
<li>For pills: add some soda or water to dissolve the pills.</li>
<li>For liquid: add something inedible like cayenne pepper, dirt, or cat litter.</li>
<li>Put the medicines in a leak-proof container like a coffee can.</li>
<li>Place the bottle or blister packs inside an opaque container like plastic laundry bottle or coffee can.</li>
<li>Close the lid and secure with packaging or duct tape</li>
<li>Hide the container in the trash</li>
<li>Dispose of the trash as close to pick-up day as possible.</li>
</ul>


<h3 id="Week-12-–-Liquidating-Assets-After-Your-Death"><strong><strong>STEP </strong>12 – LIQUIDATING ASSETS AFTER YOUR DEATH</strong></h3>

<p>Liquidating assets after your death can be a problem with your beneficiaries. It is important to leave instructions on your funeral planning guide on how you want your assets to be distributed to your beneficiaries to avoid this issue.</p>

<figure><img decoding="async" loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Week-12-Liquidating-Assets-1024x536.png" alt=""></figure>

<p><strong>Tips on how to liquidate your assets after you pass away:</strong></p>

<p>1. Prepare a will. A will can guide your beneficiaries on the proper estate liquidation. If you have real properties and vehicles, the will dictates who takes it.</p>

<p>2. Create detailed instructions on what you want to do with your properties. Come up with an objective way to divide your properties without creating a rift between your family members.</p>

<p>3. Appoint an executor on your will.</p>

<p>4. Leave detailed instructions on what to do with your unwanted stuff.</p>
