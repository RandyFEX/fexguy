<h1>Burial Insurance with Graves’ Disease</h1>

<p>Getting burial insurance with Graves’ disease is usually easier than people expect, especially if your condition is well-controlled. Burial insurance is a type of whole life insurance designed to cover funeral costs, and many people with stable thyroid levels can still qualify for first-day coverage. Insurance companies focus more on how controlled your condition is, not just the diagnosis itself. If your symptoms are unstable or you have complications, you may face higher costs or be pushed into plans with waiting periods.</p>

<h2 id="best-option-if-you-have-graves’-disease"><br><strong><strong>What is my best insurance option if I have a history of Graves’ disease?</strong></strong></h2>

<p><br><strong>THYROID SURGERY 24 MONTHS OR LONGER</strong></p>

<p>If you have Graves’ disease, there is only one thing that may affect your life insurance eligibility. The time since you had a thyroidectomy (a surgical procedure to remove the thyroid gland) may affect the type of plan you will qualify for.</p>

<p>Most people looking for affordable burial insurance after getting a thyroidectomy are happy to find out that many companies will offer a level death benefit plan with first-day coverage.</p>

<p>You will be covered from the first day and have no waiting period. Your beneficiary will receive a 100% death benefit when you pass away.</p>

<p>Graves’ disease on its own will not create an issue with the
insurer unless you have other significant medical conditions that may prevent
you from qualifying for a level death benefit plan.</p>

<p>With a level death benefit plan, whatever happens to you, as long as you pay your premiums on time, your Beneficiaries are sure to receive a death benefit payout when you die.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>RECENT THYROID SURGERY (less than 12 months)</strong></p>

<p>If you have a recent thyroidectomy (less than 12 months), some insurance companies that care about surgeries and hospitalization will offer you a first-day benefit plan.</p>

<p>You will be covered from the first day, and your death benefit will be phased in over time.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> First-day benefit plan</p>

<h2 id="do-i-need-medical-exam"><br><strong>Do I need a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You are NOT required to take a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance with Graves’ disease.</p>

<p>When you apply for burial insurance, you only have to answer some basic questions about your health. The application process is simple; you don’t need to provide medical records or blood and urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the official approval from the insurance company often within minutes!</p>

<h2 id="burial-insurance-underwriting"><br><strong>Burial insurance underwriting if you have Graves’ disease</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Burial insurance companies have two ways of underwriting:</strong></p>

<p><strong>FIRST</strong> – They may ask you a series of health questions. Your answers to their questions will determine your eligibility.</p>

<p><strong>SECOND</strong> – They will electronically review your prescription history to verify your health.</p>

<p><br><strong>HEALTH QUESTIONS:</strong></p>

<p>The insurance companies will generally ask you some health questions as part of the underwriting to determine your level of risk. </p>

<ul>
<li>Within the past 12 months, have you been advised to have surgery, hospitalization, or diagnostic test?</li>
<li>Within the past 24 months have you been hospitalized for surgery?</li>
<li>Within the past 24 months have you been medically diagnosed as having or been treated for an autoimmune disorder?</li>
<li>Within the past 2 years have you been treated for, or been advised to have treatment for immune system disorder?</li>
</ul>

<p>Review any health questions carefully if you see a question that asks if you ever have an immune system disorder. If you have had it, you should answer yes because Graves’ disease is an autoimmune disorder. If you have had a thyroidectomy, you should respond yes to the surgery question.</p>

<p>Don’t try to hide your medical condition. Burial insurance companies generally have very lenient underwriting for this health issue. Most people with health issues and medications will still qualify for the best pricing.</p>

<p><br><strong>PRESCRIPTION HISTORY CHECK</strong></p>

<p>Some companies treat Graves’ disease as a high-risk condition if you have a prescription medication history with radioactive iodine, Propylthiouracil, or Methimazole. If they find those medications in your prescription history, they may deny your application or impose a waiting period on your plan.</p>

<p>However, we work with burial insurance companies that don’t even care about Graves’ disease or Graves’ disease medication if they are considered maintenance medications. A well-managed Graves disease or hyperthyroidism does not pose a serious concern in burial insurance underwriting.</p>

<p>We work with dozens of life insurance carriers, and we can point you to those insurers that don’t care about Graves’ disease.</p>
