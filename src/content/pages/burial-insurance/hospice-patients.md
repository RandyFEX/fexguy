<h1>Burial Insurance for Hospice Patients [Use Caution]</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong><br>• Hospice patients usually can’t qualify for most life insurance policies<br>• Guaranteed issue burial insurance is often the only option available<br>• These plans cost more and delay payouts for 2 years<br>• Coverage amounts are small and only meant for final expenses<br>• Applying late limits options and locking in higher premiums<br>• Waiting periods can leave your family with less than expected</p>

<p>Burial insurance for hospice patients is extremely limited because hospice care signals a serious, end-of-life condition. Most traditional policies, like term or simplified issue whole life, will decline you. That leaves burial insurance, a type of whole life insurance, usually in guaranteed-issue form. These plans don’t ask health questions and approve almost everyone, but they come with higher costs and a 2-year waiting period before full benefits pay out. If death occurs during that period, the payout is often limited to the premiums plus interest. That tradeoff is what most people don’t realize until it’s too late.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option-if-you-have-hospice-patients"><br><strong>What Is My Best Insurance Option If I’m A Hospice Patient?</strong></h2>

<p>No insurance company will offer you first-day burial insurance coverage after being diagnosed with a short time to live.&#160;</p>

<p>If you are in hospice, your only option for insurance coverage is guaranteed issue life insurance. Guaranteed issue life insurance is available but has a 2-year waiting period, so this is also not a great plan for people entering hospice.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<h2 id="why-don’t-recommend-guaranteed-issue-insurance"><br><strong>Why We Don’t Recommend Guaranteed Issue Burial Insurance For Hospice Patients</strong></h2>

<p>The number one reason why we don’t recommend guaranteed issue life insurance for people in hospice is the two-year waiting period.&#160;</p>

<p>If your doctor thinks you’ll die in the next six months, buying guaranteed issue (GI) burial insurance will not help your family much.&#160;</p>

<p>GI policies only offer immediate first-day coverage for accidental death. There is also a mandatory two-year waiting period for any medical or health-related cause of death.</p>

<p>You must live for the first two years before your policy pays for a medical-related cause of death. This requirement makes guaranteed issue life insurance a terrible choice for people in hospice or people that are expected to die in the next 24 months.</p>

<p>This two-year waiting period is the insurance company’s way of not paying money to people who have procrastinated purchasing this insurance and are literally on their deathbed trying to buy burial insurance.</p>

<p>If you pass away during the waiting period for any reason other than an accident, your beneficiary will not receive the full death benefit. They will only receive the Return of Premium (ROP) plus interest, usually 5-10%.</p>

<p>If you die from an accident, the policy will pay your beneficiaries a 100% death benefit, even if you bought the policy recently.</p>

<h2 id="do-i-need-medical-exam-to-qualify"><br><strong>Do I Need A Medical Exam To Qualify For Burial Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You are NOT required to take a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance for hospice patients.</p>

<p>When you apply for burial insurance, you only have to answer some basic questions about your health. The application process is simple; you don’t need to provide medical records or blood and urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the official approval from the insurance company often within minutes!</p>

<h2 id="determining-your-insurance-coverage-needs"><br><strong>How Much Insurance Do I Need If I’m A Hospice Patient?</strong></h2>

<p>The first step to figuring out how much burial insurance you need is to know your end-of-life expenses. Your funeral cost is often the biggest single expense you need to pay. Other end-of-life expenses to consider are your outstanding medical bills, living expenses, credit card bills, and other debts.</p>

<h2 id="who-should-buy-guaranteed-issue-life-insurance"><br><strong>Who Should Buy Guaranteed Issue Life Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>We only sell guaranteed issue life insurance under these circumstances:</p>

<ul>
<li>They are not in hospice, or been advised to enter hospice</li>
<li>The applicant has a life expectancy of two years or longer.</li>
<li>The applicant has medical conditions that make them uninsurable for traditional life insurance.</li>
<li>When the guaranteed issue is the most affordable policy because of their health.</li>
<li>When applicants insist on no health questions asked life insurance application.</li>
</ul>

<p>Guaranteed issue life insurance can provide essential insurance coverage to some people who need insurance but don’t have other options, but it is not appropriate if you are a hospice patient.</p>

<p>If you have any of these conditions, buying guaranteed issue burial insurance is often your best option:</p>

<h2 id="non-burial-insurance-options-for-hospice-patients"><br><strong>Non-burial Insurance Options For Hospice Patients</strong></h2>

<p>If you are in hospice care and cannot qualify for life insurance, here are your non-burial insurance options:</p>

<p><strong>1. Prepaid Funeral Plan</strong></p>

<p>You have the option to take care of your funeral arrangements in advance. Instead of buying burial insurance, you can buy a prepaid funeral plan to cover your funeral and burial expenses. </p>

<p>You can buy a prepaid funeral plan at your nearest funeral home. Be warned, though. The final expenses are not cheap. Be prepared to pay between $8,000 and $15,000 to cover your final expenses.</p>

<p>If you choose this option, you need to immediately come up with the money because you must pay it in full.</p>

<p><br><strong>2. Savings Accounts</strong></p>

<p>Since you cannot qualify for burial insurance, you can open a savings account or joint savings account to deposit money to take care of your funeral and final expenses.</p>

<p>If you choose this option, you need to deposit around $8,000 to $15,000 in your account so it could be enough to cover your final expenses.</p>

<p>When you pass away, your loved ones can immediately access the funds in your savings account to pay for all the expenses associated with your death.</p>

<p><br><strong>3. Totten Trust (Payable on Death Account)</strong></p>

<p>If you don’t have burial insurance, you can opt to open a Payable On Death (POD) account. POD allows you to name a beneficiary on your bank account. Your beneficiary can access the funds on your account upon your death to pay for your final expenses.</p>

<p>This option is a bit harder, especially for fixed or limited income.</p>

<h2 id="when-is-the-best-time-to-buy-burial-insurance"><br><strong>When Is The Best Time To Buy Burial Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You should buy burial insurance just before you become a hospice patient. </p>

<p>Waiting to buy burial insurance when you are in hospice is what procrastinators and poor planners do. Everyone knows they will die someday, so don’t wait until it’s too late to buy your burial, cremation, or final expense life insurance.</p>

<p>The best time to buy burial insurance is NOW! The younger you are, the lower your rates will be.&#160;</p>

<p>The healthier you are, the lower your rates will be.&#160;</p>

<h2 id="benefits-of-burial-&-funeral-insurance"><br><strong>Benefits Of Burial &amp; Funeral Insurance</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Here are some of the benefits of purchasing a burial or funeral policy:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>No medical exam or doctor’s visit is required</strong>&#160;– easy to get approved.</li>
<li><strong>Ease of issue</strong>&#160;– easy to qualify and get insurance coverage.</li>
<li><strong>No Money Down to get approved&#160;</strong>– have your policy start whenever you want.</li>
<li><strong>Level premium</strong>&#160;– your premium will never increase.</li>
<li><strong>Fixed death benefit</strong>&#160;– your death benefit will never decrease for any reason.</li>
<li><strong>Permanent protection</strong>&#160;– your policy can not be canceled by the life insurance company if you continue to pay your premiums.</li>
<li><strong>Tax-free</strong>&#160;– the death benefit is directly paid to your beneficiary tax-free upon your death.</li>
