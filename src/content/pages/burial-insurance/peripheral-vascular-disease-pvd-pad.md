<h1>Burial Insurance with Peripheral Vascular Disease (PVD or PAD)</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong><br>• PVD or PAD won’t automatically disqualify you from burial insurance<br>• Severe cases or recent surgery can trigger higher rates or delays<br>• Some people still qualify for first-day coverage if stable long enough<br>• Guaranteed issue plans cost more and delay payouts for 2 years<br>• Many assume denial after one rejection when other companies would approve<br>• Not comparing carriers can lock you into expensive, limited coverage</p>

<p>Burial insurance with peripheral vascular disease (PVD or PAD) is more available than most people think, but it depends on how serious and stable your condition is. Burial insurance, which is a type of whole life insurance, is often designed for higher-risk health cases, so many people can still qualify. If your condition is well-managed or your surgery was over 2 years ago, some companies may even offer first-day coverage. If it’s recent or severe, you’ll likely face higher costs or be pushed into guaranteed issue plans with waiting periods. The difference comes down to timing, severity, and choosing the right company.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option-if-you-have-peripheral-vascular-disease"><br><strong>What Is My Best Insurance Option If I Have A History Of Peripheral Vascular Disease?</strong></h2>

<p><strong>EARLY-STAGE PERIPHERAL VASCULAR DISEASE</strong></p>

<p>If your peripheral vascular disease is still in the early stage where you only use medications to treat your condition, the best option for you is a level death benefit plan with first-day coverage. </p>

<p>You will be covered from the first day, and your beneficiary will receive your full death benefit when you pass away.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option</strong>: Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage.</p>

<p><br><strong>ADVANCED PERIPHERAL VASCULAR DISEASE</strong></p>

<p>The advanced stage of peripheral vascular disease requires more aggressive forms of treatment. Surgery will be necessary to treat the complications of PVD. </p>

<p><strong>The common surgeries to treat PVD or PAD include:</strong></p>

<ol>
<li><a href="/burial-insurance/heart-surgery/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Angioplasty</a> – helps open up the artery and increase blood flow to the heart.</li>
<li>Bypass grafting – a graft is placed around the blocked artery to restore blood flow to the heart.</li>
<li><a href="/burial-insurance/stent/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Stent implant</a> – small mesh wires are inserted into the diseased artery to prevent it from re-closing and to prevent blood clots from forming in the artery.</li>
</ol>

<p>If you had any of these procedures to treat your condition, it is crucial to remember that when you had the&#160;<a href="https://www.webmd.com/heart-disease/treatments-peripheral-artery-disease#1" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">surgery</a>&#160;will determine what type of burial insurance you will qualify for.</p>

<p><br><strong>IF YOUR SURGERY WAS OVER 2 YEARS NOW</strong></p>

<p>If your surgery is over two years, you will have the best-priced options available.</p>

<p>Burial insurance companies will offer you a level death benefit plan with first-day coverage at the lowest possible premium.&#160;You will be treated like you never had surgery in the first place!</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong>&#160;Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>IF YOUR SURGERY IS WITHIN 12 MONTHS</strong></p>

<p>You will qualify for a first-day benefit plan if you have had any surgical procedures in the last 12 months. No insurance company would offer you first-day coverage if your surgery were within the last year.</p>

<p>The first-day benefit plan will cover you from the first day, and your death benefit will be phased in over time.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong>&#160;First-day benefit plan</p>

<p><br><strong>AMPUTATION</strong></p>

<p>Having an amputation due to peripheral artery disease can also affect your life insurance eligibility. If your peripheral vascular disease caused you to have an amputation, your best insurance option is a first-day benefit plan.</p>

<p>You will be covered from the first day, and your death benefit will be phased in over time.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> First-day benefit plan</p>

<p><br><strong>SMOKING</strong></p>

<p>Avoiding smoking increases your risk of developing a peripheral vascular disease. Smoking directly causes reduced blood flow in the arteries. It also causes PVD to get worse, increasing your risk of stroke and heart attack.</p>

<p>Smoking is a significant factor in determining your risk, and life insurance rates for smokers are 200 to 300% higher than non-smoker rates.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> If you are a non-smoker, you will qualify for discounted pricing</p>

<h2 id="do-i-need-medical-exam-to-qualify"><br><strong>If I Have Peripheral Vascular Disease, Do I Need A Medical Exam To Qualify For Burial Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You are NOT required to take a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance with peripheral vascular disease or peripheral artery disease.</p>

<p>When you apply for burial insurance, you only have to answer basic questions about your health. The application process is simple; you don’t need to provide medical records or blood and urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the official approval from the insurance company often within minutes!</p>

<h2 id="insurance-underwriting-for-peripheral-vascular-disease"><br><strong>Burial Insurance Underwriting If You Have Peripheral Vascular Disease</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Burial insurance companies have two ways of underwriting:</strong></p>

<p><strong>FIRST</strong>&#160;– They may ask you a series of health questions. Your answers to their questions will determine your eligibility.</p>

<p><strong>SECOND</strong>&#160;– They will electronically review your prescription history to verify your health.</p>

<p><br><strong>HEALTH QUESTIONS</strong></p>

<p>Burial insurance with underwriting will ask you health questions. To give you an idea, here are some sample questions you might see on the application that asks about peripheral vascular disease:</p>

<ol>
<li>In the past 12 months, have you been diagnosed or treated with a peripheral vascular disease?</li>
<li>Have you ever received treatment for peripheral vascular disease or peripheral artery disease?</li>
<li>In the past two years have you been diagnosed or treated, or been prescribed medication, or had circulatory surgery?</li>
<li>In the past two years, have you been prescribed medication or treated for cardiovascular disorder or stroke?</li>
</ol>

<p><br><strong>PRESCRIPTION HISTORY CHECK</strong></p>

<p>Burial insurance companies will electronically check your prescription history to check for medications for peripheral vascular disease and other medical conditions.</p>

<p>Medications used to treat peripheral vascular disease depend on the underlying cause of your PVD or PAD. Medications are used to lower the risk of atherosclerosis in the arteries.</p>

<p><strong>Here’s a list of common medications for peripheral vascular disease or peripheral artery disease:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Aspirin</li>
<li>Atorvastatin</li>
<li>Cilostazol</li>
<li>Clopidogrel</li>
<li>Dipyridamole</li>
<li>Naftidrofuryl oxalate</li>
<li>Pentoxifylline</li>
<li>Simvastatin</li>
<li>Ticlopidine</li>
</ul>

<p>If you are taking any of these prescription drugs, you will be considered a PVD patient this may affect how much you pay and if you will have a waiting period.</p>
