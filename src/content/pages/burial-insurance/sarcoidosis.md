<h1>Burial Insurance with Sarcoidosis</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong></p>

<p>• Mild sarcoidosis can still qualify for first-day coverage with the right company<br>• Severe or active cases often get pushed into 2-year waiting period plans<br>• Applying with the wrong carrier can lead to unnecessary declines or higher costs<br>• Your organ involvement and stability matter more than the diagnosis itself<br>• Guaranteed issue plans cost more and delay full payout if you die early</p>

<p>Getting burial insurance with sarcoidosis depends on how serious and stable your condition is. Many people can still qualify for whole life or burial insurance with immediate coverage if the disease is mild and well-controlled. But if symptoms are active, involve multiple organs, or require heavy treatment, you’re more likely to get placed into a guaranteed issue policy with higher costs and a 2-year waiting period. Insurance companies don’t just look at the name “sarcoidosis.” They look at severity, treatment, and stability over time. That’s why applying the right way matters.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option-if-you-have-sarcoidosis"><br><strong>What Is My Best Insurance Option If I Have A History Of Sarcoidosis?</strong></h2>

<p>The type of burial insurance with sarcoidosis you will qualify for will depend on the severity of your condition.</p>

<p><strong>MILD SARCOIDOSIS</strong></p>

<p>Mild sarcoidosis is never a severe medical condition because it usually goes away on its own. Typically, treatment is minimal and may consist of only taking a few medications as prescribed. Because of its treatability, most life insurance providers consider mild sarcoidosis as low risk. </p>

<p>Having mild sarcoidosis qualifies you for first-day coverage with most life insurance companies.</p>

<p>The level death benefit is the best policy option because it offers the most substantial death benefit, and it has no waiting period. This is the policy we always try to get our clients.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option: </strong>Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>MODERATE SARCOIDOSIS</strong></p>

<p>Moderate sarcoidosis requiring minimal treatment may qualify
you for first-day full coverage with some burial insurance companies. </p>

<p>If you have no other health problems, there is an excellent chance of qualifying for the lowest rate. You must show that your sarcoidosis went away, or you’re only taking minimal treatment.&#160; </p>

<p>As a standalone medical condition, Sarcoidosis will not create an issue, especially if it’s mild to moderate unless you have other health conditions that may prevent you from qualifying for first-day full coverage.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option: </strong>Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>SEVERE SARCOIDOSIS</strong></p>

<p>Sarcoidosis patients with severe symptoms or complications because of this condition will be offered guaranteed issue burial insurance. This is also the plan you will likely be offered if your sarcoidosis is under control, but you have other severe health problems.</p>

<p>It’s quite rare to be offered a guaranteed issue policy for sarcoidosis. You must have a severe complication like supplemental oxygen use or needing help with activities of daily living to qualify for GI policy.</p>

<p>As with all qualification requirements for burial insurance, it all comes down to your overall health and potential complications.</p>

<p>If you are a U.S citizen or permanent resident between the ages of 40 to 85, you can be eligible for guaranteed issue burial insurance.</p>

<p>Guaranteed issue burial insurance comes with a graded death benefit that imposes a two-year waiting period. You will need to live two years before being fully covered for the natural cause of death. GI policy will only pay full death benefit during the waiting period for accidental death.</p>

<p><strong>Best Options:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p><br><strong>SARCOIDOSIS AND SUPPLEMENTAL OXYGEN</strong></p>

<p>In most cases, sarcoidosis develops in the lungs, and
patients have difficulty breathing, and their doctor prescribed supplemental
oxygen. Every life insurance company asks about supplemental oxygen in the
health questionnaire. There is only one exception, and that is the use of
supplemental oxygen for sleep apnea.</p>

<p>You will typically see supplemental oxygen asked this way: </p>

<p>“Within the previous 12 months, have you been advised to use supplemental oxygen to assist with breathing (excluding use for sleep apnea).”</p>

<p>It will be challenging to qualify for a level death benefit
or first-day coverage if you need oxygen equipment to breathe. No Insurance
company offers first-day coverage to those who are using supplemental oxygen.
However, you will be eligible for a guaranteed issue burial insurance plan.</p>

<p><strong>Best Options:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

Granulomas formed in the brain and the spinal cord can cause paralysis. <a href="/burial-insurance-paralysis-paralyzed/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Paralysis</a>, which will require you to need hospice care or help with <a href="/burial-insurance-adl-activities-of-daily-living/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">activities of daily living</a>, will affect your life insurance eligibility.</li>
