<h1>Burial Insurance for Terminal Illness [Use Caution]</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong><br>• Terminal illness usually disqualifies you from most life insurance policies<br>• Guaranteed issue burial insurance is often your only real option<br>• These plans cost more and delay payouts for 2 years<br>• Coverage amounts are small and meant only for final expenses<br>• Buying late limits options and locks in higher premiums<br>• Not understanding waiting periods can leave your family with less</p>

<p>Burial insurance with a terminal illness is limited but still possible. Most traditional policies, like term or simplified issue whole life, will decline you after a terminal diagnosis. That leaves guaranteed-issue burial insurance, a type of whole life insurance designed for high-risk cases. These plans don’t ask health questions and almost always approve you, but they come with higher costs and a 2-year waiting period before full benefits pay out. Burial insurance is often the last option to cover funeral costs when other policies aren’t available, which is why timing matters so much.</p>

<h2 id="why-we-don’t-recommend-guaranteed-issue-insurance"><br><strong>Why I Don’t Recommend Guaranteed Issue Burial Insurance For Terminal Illness Patients</strong></h2>

<p><strong>1. 2-Year Waiting Period</strong></p>

<p>The #1 reason why I don’t recommend the guaranteed issue life insurance for a terminal illness is the waiting period. <strong>This plan does not offer immediate coverage other than for accidental death during the first two years.</strong></p>

<p>You will automatically be approved for coverage with no questions asked when you take out this plan. Your plan will also have a mandatory two-year waiting period for any health or medical-related cause of death.</p>

<p>The two-year waiting period is a financial safety net for the insurance company. It is their way of protecting themselves from paying a lot of money to people who are literally on their deathbeds and trying to buy life insurance.</p>

<p>This policy will require you to live for the first two years before your policy pays for any health or medical-related cause of death. If you pass away during the waiting period, your beneficiary will not get your full death benefit. Instead, they will get a Return Of Premium (ROP) plus interest (usually 7-10%)</p>

<p>For example, your monthly premium is $100. If you paid one year before passing away, your beneficiary would receive your $1,200 premium plus $120 to account for the 10% interest for a total of $1,320.</p>

<p>The company will pay your full death benefit if you die from accidental death during the waiting period. It’s only for medical or health causes of death where the first-day coverage doesn’t take effect until after the two-year waiting period.</p>

<p><br><strong>2. More Expensive</strong></p>

<p>Dollar for dollar, <strong>a guaranteed issue, life insurance is up to three to five times more expensive than life insurance with medical underwriting.</strong> You will pay more on your policy because of the additional risk the life insurance company takes for approving your policy without asking any medical questions.</p>

<p><br><strong>3. Limited Death Benefit Option</strong></p>

<p><strong>Guaranteed issue life insurance coverage maxes out at $25,000.</strong> That’s the highest coverage you can buy. If you’re planning to buy a life insurance policy to pay off your mortgage or pay for your children’s college education, this plan will not work for you.</p>

<h2 id="do-you-need-medical-exam-to-qualify"><br><strong>Do I Need A Medical Exam To Qualify For Burial Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You are NOT required to take a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance for terminal illness.</p>

<p>The guaranteed issue burial insurance application process is simple, and you don’t need to provide medical records or blood and urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the official approval from the insurance company often within minutes!</p>

<h2 id="best-time-to-get-burial-insurance"><br><strong><strong>When Is The Best Time To Buy Burial Insurance?</strong></strong></h2>

<p>I often get calls from people asking for burial insurance because their loved ones have a terminal illness. It’s heartbreaking to tell them that it’s too late and that they have waited too long.</p>

<p>Waiting until you have a terminal illness before buying burial insurance is like trying to buy a parachute after you have jumped out of a plane…it’s not a good idea to procrastinate.</p>

<p>The best time to get life insurance is before you are sick or ill, as your health is a determining factor for insurability from first-day coverage companies.&#160;</p>

<p>If you’re considering getting burial insurance, do it NOW before it’s too late! Do it now while you can still lock in the lowest pricing!&#160;</p>

<p>To get the lowest price on life insurance, buy it when you are younger rather than older. The older you are, the more expensive your premium will be.</p>

<p>Buy it when you are healthier rather than with significant health problems. The healthier you are, the lower your premiums will be.</p>

<h2 id="alternative-options-if-you-have-terminal-illness"><br><strong>What Are The Life Insurance Alternative Options For People With Terminal Illness?</strong></h2>

<p>Insurance is about risk management; insurance companies see terminal illness life insurance as a poor business decision.&#160;</p>

<p>If you have a terminal illness that prevents you from qualifying for insurance, you have three other options.</p>

<p><strong>1. Prepaid Funeral Plan</strong></p>

<p>You can pay for your funeral and burial in advance. You have the option to buy a prepaid funeral plan and make arrangements before your death.&#160;</p>

<p>You don’t need a burial insurance policy to cover your final expenses when you sign up for a prepaid funeral plan.&#160;</p>

<p>Be warned! Funeral and final expenses are not cheap. If you choose this option, be prepared to pay between $8,000 and $15,000.</p>

<p>Prepaid plans are not great options for people with terminal illnesses because they must be paid off for your funeral.</p>

<p><br><strong>2. Savings Accounts</strong></p>

<p>Instead of buying burial insurance, you can open a savings account, or joint savings account to deposit money to take care of your final expenses.&#160;</p>

<p>When you die, your family can immediately access the funds from this account to pay for your funeral, burial, and other costs associated with your death.</p>

<p>Savings accounts are not a great option for people with terminal illnesses since all of the people I help are on fixed or limited incomes and don’t have $8,000 to $15,000 in their savings accounts.</p>

<p><br><strong>3. Payable on Death Account (Totten Trust)</strong></p>

<p>Opening a Payable On Death <a href="https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/free-books/avoid-probate-book/chapter1-1.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">(POD)</a> account is your other option if you can’t get life insurance. POD allows you to name a beneficiary on your bank account.</p>

<p>The funds on your account can be used for your funeral. Upon your death, your beneficiary can immediately access the funds to pay for your funeral expenses.</p>

<p>Since most people ages 50-85 are on fixed or limited incomes, starting a POD account is difficult, if not impossible, for the people I help daily.</p>
