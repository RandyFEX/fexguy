<h1>Burial Insurance with Melanoma [Skin Cancer]</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong><br>• Melanoma can limit your options or delay approval for coverage<br>• Recent treatment often pushes you into 2-year waiting period plans<br>• Advanced cases can lead to denial or very high premiums<br>• Applying too soon can cost you better policy options<br>• The wrong company can overcharge you for the same coverage</p>

<p>Getting burial insurance with melanoma skin cancer depends heavily on timing and how serious your condition is. Burial insurance is a type of whole life insurance designed to cover funeral costs, even if you’ve had cancer. If your melanoma was caught early and treated years ago, you may still qualify for first-day coverage. If it’s recent or advanced, most companies will limit you to plans with a 2-year waiting period or higher costs. The mistake is applying too early or with the wrong company, which can cost you better coverage.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option-if-you-have-melanoma"><br><strong>What is my best insurance option if I have a history of melanoma?</strong></h2>

<p>Your ability to qualify for burial insurance with melanoma depends on the following factors:</p>

<ul>
<li>Date of your melanoma diagnosis</li>
<li>How long has passed since your melanoma treatment</li>
<li>Prescription medications taken</li>
<li>Amount of time cancer-free.</li>
</ul>

<p><br><strong>MELANOMA TREATMENTS </strong></p>

<p>There are many types of skin cancer treatments that the insurance companies consider before offering approval or offer of coverage:</p>

<ol>
<li>Surgery</li>
<li>Radiation</li>
<li>Chemotherapy</li>
<li>Immunotherapy</li>
<li>Targeted therapy (chemotherapy and radiation)</li>
<li>Any prescription drugs meant to treat cancer</li>
</ol>

<p>If you are undergoing any of these because of skin cancer, these will qualify as a treatment for cancer.</p>

<p><strong>YOU HAVE HAD MELANOMA TREATMENT AND CANCER-FREE FOR 2 YEARS</strong> <strong>OR LONGER</strong></p>

<p>You are considered cancer-free by most burial insurance companies after two years have passed since your last treatment. </p>

<p>It’s unusual for a final expense company to ask about melanoma or skin cancer beyond the two-year mark, although some companies look back three years. You may qualify for level death benefit with first-day coverage and no waiting period on most final expense companies.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option</strong>: First-day coverage plan</p>

<p><br><strong>YOU HAVE MELANOMA AND YOU HAVE BEEN TREATED WITHIN THE LAST TWO YEARS</strong></p>

<p>Unfortunately, most burial insurance companies have a two-year look-back period on melanoma. Because of this, you fall into the highest risk category with every company.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance with a waiting period</p>

<p><strong>Important note</strong>: You cannot avoid a waiting period if you have been treated for melanoma within the last two years. If you pass during the waiting period, your beneficiaries will not receive the full death benefit; instead, they will receive the refund of all your premiums plus interest.</p>

<p><strong>Melanoma vs. basal and squamous cell skin cancer:</strong> Melanoma is treated as a serious cancer in underwriting. Previously treated <a href="/burial-insurance/basal-cell-squamous-cell-carcinoma/">basal cell and squamous cell skin cancers</a> are underwritten differently and generally do not prevent first-day coverage.</p>

<h2 id="do-i-need-medical-exam-to-qualify"><br><strong>Do I need a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You are NOT required to take a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance with melanoma.</p>

<p>When you apply for burial insurance, you only have to answer basic questions about your health. The application process is simple; you don’t need to provide medical records or blood and urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the official approval from the insurance company often within minutes!</p>

<h2 id="insurance-underwriting-for-melanoma"><br><strong>Burial insurance underwriting if you have melanoma</strong></h2>

<p>Every single burial insurance company will ask about cancer in their application. Each company has unique underwriting guidelines, which consider some health and medical variables when underwriting your application.</p>

<p>They will ask you these questions to determine what policy options are open for you and how much your monthly premium will be. They will also check your prescription history as part of the underwriting process.</p>

<p id="HEALTH-QUESTIONS-ON-YOUR-FUNERAL-OR-BURIAL-INSURANCE-APPLICATION"><br><strong>HEALTH QUESTIONS </strong></p>

<p>The first part of the burial insurance underwriting process is the health questionnaire.</p>

<p><strong>You can see melanoma (skin cancer) asked this way:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>In the last 2 years, have you been diagnosed with cancer or been advised to receive radiation or chemotherapy for cancer?</li>
<li>In the last 2 years, have you ever been treated for internal cancer?</li>
<li>In the last 3 years, have you received treatment for any internal cancer?</li>
<li>In the last 3 years, have you been diagnosed with cancer or received radiation or chemotherapy for cancer?</li>
</ul>

<p>Melanoma has a two-year minimum lookback period. If you have had melanoma in the last two years, you will still qualify for a higher-risk option with the insurance carrier.</p>

<p><br><strong>PRESCRIPTION HISTORY CHECK</strong></p>

<p>Burial insurance companies will check your prescription history to determine your medications for any health conditions.</p>

<p><strong>Here’s a list of some common drugs for Melanoma:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Ipilimumab</li>
<li>Nivolumab</li>
<li>Opdivo</li>
<li>Pembrolizumab</li>
<li>Sylatron</li>
<li>Trametinib</li>
<li>Yervoy</li>
</ul>

<p>There are different drugs to treat melanoma. If you have filled any cancer treatment medications within the last two years, burial insurance carriers will assume you are undergoing treatment for melanoma.</p>
