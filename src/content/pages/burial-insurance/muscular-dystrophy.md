<h1>Burial Insurance with Muscular Dystrophy</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong><br>• Muscular dystrophy won’t automatically disqualify you from burial insurance<br>• Ability to live independently is the biggest factor for approval<br>• Wheelchair use or needing help can limit you to worse plans<br>• Guaranteed issue plans cost more and delay payouts for 2 years<br>• Severe cases often get less coverage at higher monthly cost<br>• Not comparing companies can leave you stuck with expensive options</p>

<p>Burial insurance with muscular dystrophy depends on how advanced your condition is and how independent you are. If you can still handle daily activities on your own, some companies may offer burial insurance, which is a type of whole life insurance, with first-day coverage and better rates. If you use a wheelchair or need help with daily tasks, most companies will decline you and only offer guaranteed issue plans with higher costs and a 2-year waiting period. That difference is huge, and most people don’t realize how fast their options can change as the condition progresses.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option-if-you-have-muscular-dystrophy"><br><strong>What Is My Best Insurance Option If I Have A History Of Muscular Dystrophy?</strong></h2>

<p id="WHY-LIFE-INSURANCE-COMPANIES-CARE-ABOUT-MUSCULAR-DYSTROPHY"><strong>Types of Muscular Dystrophy the insurance companies want to know about:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Becker muscular dystrophy</li>
<li>Facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy</li>
<li>Oculopharyngeal muscular dystrophy</li>
<li>Myotonic muscular dystrophy</li>
<li>Distal muscular dystrophy</li>
</ul>

<p>If you suffer from any of these types of muscular dystrophy and you can perform activities of daily living on your own, your best option is a level death benefit plan with first-day coverage from select final expense companies. You would pay the lowest rates possible, and your policy will not have a waiting period.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p id="OTHER-FACTORS-THAT-MAY-AFFECT-UNDERWRITING"><br><strong>OTHER FACTORS THAT MAY AFFECT ELIGIBILITY</strong></p>

<p><strong>WHEELCHAIR USE</strong></p>

<p>Many applicants with muscular dystrophy start using <a href="/burial-insurance/wheelchair-users/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">wheelchairs</a> when they lose muscle mass and their muscles weaken. Most burial insurance companies ask about wheelchair use.</p>

<p>If your medical condition gets worse and you need to use a wheelchair for mobility, or you need assistance with activities of daily living, guaranteed issue life insurance is your only option to get a policy. This is a no-health questions policy, and you are guaranteed acceptance regardless of your medical condition.</p>

<p>Guaranteed issue policies cost a little bit more than a level death benefit and come with a two-year waiting period.</p>

<p>If you pass away from a natural cause during the first two years of the policy, your beneficiaries will get the return of premiums plus interest, typically 10%.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p><br><strong>ASSISTANCE WITH ACTIVITIES OF DAILY LIVING</strong></p>

<p>Every final expense life insurance company always asks if you need help in performing <a href="/burial-insurance/adl-activities-of-daily-living/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">activities of daily living</a>. If you have a severe type of muscular dystrophy, you will need help with ADLs as your condition gets worse.</p>

<p>Insurance companies define assistance with daily living activities as needing help with the following: <a href="/burial-insurance/eating-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">eating</a>, <a href="/burial-insurance/bathing-disability-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">bathing</a>, <a href="/burial-insurance/need-help-with-dressing-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">dressing</a>, <a href="/burial-insurance/toileting-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">toileting</a>, <a href="/burial-insurance/transferring-activities-of-daily-living-adl/">transferring</a>, and <a href="/burial-insurance/continence-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">continence</a>.</p>

<p>Most burial insurance companies will decline your application in this situation, and getting a guaranteed issue life insurance is your only option (and something we can help you with).</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p><br><strong>HOME HEALTH CARE</strong></p>

<p>This scenario is identical to needing help with daily living activities.</p>

<p>There are only a few companies that accept home health care applicants. If you need permanent home health care, your best option to get coverage is to get a guaranteed issue burial insurance.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<h2 id="do-i-need-medical-exam-to-qualify"><br><strong>Do I Need A Medical Exam To Qualify For Burial Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You are NOT required to take a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance with muscular dystrophy.</p>

<p>When you apply for burial insurance, you only have to answer some basic questions about your health. The application process is simple, and you don’t need to provide medical records or blood and urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the official approval from the insurance company often within minutes!</p>

<h2 id="insurance-underwriting-for-muscular-dystrophy"><br><strong><strong>Burial Insurance Underwriting If You Have Muscular Dystrophy</strong></strong></h2>

<p><strong>Burial insurance companies have two ways of underwriting:</strong></p>

<p><strong>FIRST</strong> – They may ask you a series of health questions. Your answers to their questions will determine your eligibility.</p>

<p><strong>SECOND</strong> – They will electronically review your prescription history to verify your health.</p>

<p><br><strong>HEALTH QUESTIONS</strong></p>

<p>Burial and funeral insurance companies with underwriting questions will ask questions about your health to assess your level of risk. They will ask about muscular dystrophy in different ways.</p>

<p><strong>Here is an example of how they might ask about it:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>During the past 24 months, have you been treated for neuromuscular disease?</li>
<li>Within the past 24 months, have you been medically diagnosed or treated for any neuromuscular disease?</li>
<li>In the last 24 months, did you have muscular dystrophy or motor neuron disease?</li>
</ul>

<p>Regardless of how they ask about muscular dystrophy, you should say yes to these questions if you have been diagnosed with this condition.</p>

<p><br><strong>PRESCRIPTION HISTORY CHECK</strong></p>

<p>Aside from the health questions, final expense insurance companies will electronically review your prescription history to verify your health history and current health status.</p>

<p>For example, if you have muscular dystrophy but didn’t disclose it in the health questionnaire, your prescription records and past prescription history will confirm your diagnosis.</p>

<p><strong>Here is a list of the most common medications that are flagged for muscular dystrophy:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Corticosteroids (Prednisone)</li>
<li>Emflaza (Deflazacort)</li>
<li>Exondys 51 (Eteplirsen)</li>
<li>Translarna (Ataluren)</li>
</ul>

<p>If you are taking any of these <a href="https://musculardystrophynews.com/muscular-dystrophy-medications/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">drugs</a>, most burial insurance companies will look at you as if you are a muscular dystrophy patient. If you answer no to the muscular dystrophy questions, the presence of these drugs in your prescription history will override your answer.</p>

<p>The first step to figuring out how much burial insurance you need is to know your end-of-life expenses. Your funeral cost is often the biggest single expense you need to pay. Other end-of-life expenses to consider are your outstanding medical bills, living expenses, credit card bills, and other debts.</p>
