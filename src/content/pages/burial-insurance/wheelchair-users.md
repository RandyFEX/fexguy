<h1>Burial Insurance for Wheelchair Users</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong></p>

<p>• Being in a wheelchair doesn’t automatically disqualify you from coverage<br>• Severe conditions can push you into higher-cost, waiting period policies<br>• Some companies treat wheelchair use as a high-risk underwriting factor<br>• Applying incorrectly can lead to declines or a more expensive plan placement<br>• First-day coverage is still possible, depending on overall health stability</p>

<p>Being in a wheelchair matters to insurance companies, but it’s not the whole story. Burial insurance companies look at <em>why</em> you’re in a wheelchair, not just the fact that you are. If it’s tied to a stable condition, you may still qualify for whole life or burial insurance with first-day coverage. If it’s tied to serious illness or limited mobility, you’re more likely to get pushed into a higher-cost policy with a 2-year waiting period. Some insurers even flag wheelchair use as a higher-risk factor during underwriting, which can limit your options if you apply the wrong way.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option-if-you’re-a-wheelchair-user"><br><strong><strong>What Is My Best Insurance Option If I’m A Wheelchair User?</strong></strong></h2>

<p>The type of burial insurance you can qualify for will depend on why you use a wheelchair and if you need assistance in activities of daily living such as eating, bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, and continence.</p>

<p><br><strong>TEMPORARY WHEELCHAIR USE</strong></p>

<p>You use a wheelchair because you have <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK143782/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">difficulty walking</a> or you are temporarily disabled. If you have a temporary disability, you may benefit from a wheelchair prescription. A person with broken bones may need a temporary wheelchair until injuries have healed.</p>

<p>If your wheelchair usage is temporary, you will qualify for a level death benefit with first-day coverage.&#160;You will be 100% covered from the first day, and no need to have a waiting period. You can also get the lowest premium.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option: </strong>Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>OCCASIONAL WHEELCHAIR USE ( able to walk without help if needed)</strong></p>

<p>You use a wheelchair occasionally, even when you can walk, because you have difficulty walking. You use a wheelchair for traveling long distances or for moving comfortably around while shopping at a local grocery store</p>

<p>Occasional wheelchair users use a wheelchair due to some health conditions. Those who easily get tired and lose energy quickly need a wheelchair to protect themselves. </p>

<p>Occasional wheelchair users can move around the house upright. They can walk independently but use a walker or cane to walk. Sometimes they will use scooters or electric wheelchairs to move independently without pain.</p>

<p>If you occasionally use a wheelchair but you can still walk independently, you could qualify for a level death benefit. You will be 100% protected from day one, and no need to have a waiting period.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option</strong>: Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>WHEELCHAIR USE (cannot walk but can independently perform all activities of daily living)</strong></p>

<p>While occasional wheelchair users use a wheelchair for moving longer distances, others who are bound to a wheelchair generally rely on them for mobility.</p>

<p><strong>There are many reasons to use a wheelchair. Some people are paralyzed by car accidents. Others may use a wheelchair, not for chronic illness but because of:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>A stroke</li>
<li><a href="/burial-insurance-paralysis-paralyzed/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Paralysis</a></li>
<li>Obesity</li>
<li>You were born with some physical impairment causing you to need a wheelchair</li>
<li>Recovering from surgery</li>
</ul>

<p>If you are using a wheelchair at home, your best life insurance option is guaranteed issue burial insurance.</p>

<p><br><strong><a href="/burial-insurance-adl-activities-of-daily-living/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">ACTIVITIES OF DAILY LIVING</a></strong></p>

<p>If you’re a wheelchair user, we need to know if you can do daily living activities independently, needing help from other people will affect your ability to qualify for the best-priced burial insurance plan.</p>

<p>Insurance companies use six daily living activities to determine if you will qualify for a level death benefit. You must be able to perform these ADLs to take care of yourself properly.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong><a href="/burial-insurance-help-with-eating-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Eating</a></strong> is putting food into your body using a plate, cup, feeding tube, or intravenously.</li>
<li><strong><a href="/burial-insurance-bathing-disability-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Bathing</a></strong> – washing your body in a shower or bathtub, it also refers to your ability to get in and out of the shower or bathtub safely.</li>
<li><strong><a href="/burial-insurance-need-help-with-dressing-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Dressing</a></strong> – taking off and putting an article of clothing on your body. It also includes taking off and putting on fasteners, braces, and artificial limbs.</li>
<li><strong><a href="/burial-insurance-help-with-toileting-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Toileting</a></strong> – getting on and off the toilet and performing necessary personal hygiene.</li>
<li><strong><a href="/burial-insurance-help-with-transferring/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Transferring</a></strong> – moving in or out of bed, a chair, or a wheelchair.</li>
<li><strong><a href="/burial-insurance-help-with-continence-activities-of-daily-living-adl/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Continence</a></strong> – controlling the bladder and bowel functions. Continence includes performing related personal hygiene tasks like caring for a colostomy or catheter bag.</li>
</ol>

<p><br><strong>WHEELCHAIR USE&#160; (no chronic illness but need help in ADL)</strong></p>

<p>When it comes to wheelchair use, the significant factor affecting insurance eligibility is why you are in a wheelchair.</p>

<p>If you need help performing any ADL, your best option is to get a guaranteed issue life insurance policy priced well. This is also the life insurance for paraplegics that need help with ADL. </p>

<p>If you fall into this category, you cannot avoid having a waiting period. The company will refund your premiums plus 10% interest if you pass away during the waiting period. </p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance<br><br><br><strong>WHEELCHAIR USE (with chronic illness with help or no help with ADL)</strong></p>

<p><strong>If you use a wheelchair due to chronic illness or disease such as:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Cerebral palsy</li>
<li>Alzheimer’s disease</li>
<li>Multiple sclerosis</li>
<li>Parkinson’s disease</li>
<li>Dementia</li>
<li>Diabetes complications</li>
</ul>

<p>You will be ineligible for any coverage that has health questions. In this case, your only path to insurance coverage is through a guaranteed issue burial insurance.</p>

<p>Whether or not you need help with activities of daily living is irrelevant if you use a wheelchair because of a chronic illness.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option</strong>: Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>
