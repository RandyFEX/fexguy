Scleroderma is a serious autoimmune condition, but some insurance companies will still approve you depending on how stable your health is. Burial insurance, which is a type of whole life insurance, is often the easiest option because it’s designed for people with medical conditions. In many cases, you can still qualify for first-day coverage, not just waiting period plans. The mistake people make is assuming all companies treat scleroderma the same, when approval and pricing can vary a lot.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option-if-you-have-scleroderma"><br><strong>What Is My Best Insurance Option If I Have A History Of Scleroderma?</strong></h2>

<p>Qualifying for burial insurance with scleroderma will depend on your type of scleroderma and the severity of your condition. Scleroderma on its own is a non-issue to most final expense insurance providers. However, scleroderma complications may affect your eligibility for the best plan.</p>

<p><br><strong>LOCALIZED SCLERODERMA</strong></p>

<p>The scleroderma is localized on the skin. This type is relatively mild and only affects the skin or muscles. Localized scleroderma is viewed as favorable because it only affects the skin on the hands and face and develops slowly. </p>

<p>You will easily qualify for a level death benefit if you have minimal to moderate scleroderma symptoms. If you have localized scleroderma, you’ll also pay the lowest rate from most final expense companies.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>SYSTEMIC SCLERODERMA</strong></p>

<p>This a condition that leads to fibrosis or tissue scarring, damage to blood vessels, and abnormal connective tissue growth in the muscles and joints. It can cause a breakdown in the skin, the tissue under it, muscles, and internal organs such as the lungs, kidneys, heart, and intestinal tract.</p>

<p>Complications from systemic scleroderma can cause difficulty in swallowing, acid reflux (GERD), ulcers, damage to bowel muscles, respiratory muscle weakness, lung scarring, pulmonary fibrosis, and kidney disease.</p>

<p>These complications from systemic scleroderma can make you pay more premiums and possibly result in a waiting period.</p>

<p>Guaranteed issue burial insurance has a two-year graded period for a natural cause of death. If you pass away during the waiting period, the insurance company will return your premiums plus 10% interest to your beneficiaries.</p>

<p><strong>Best Options:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p id="SCLERODERMA-COMPLICATIONS-AND-LIFE-INSURANCE-AVAILABILITY"><br><strong>SCLERODERMA COMPLICATIONS </strong></p>

<p>Scleroderma affects the skin and also the connective tissues and internal organs. Scleroderma may cause different complications that may affect the amount you paid and when your policy will take effect.</p>

<p><strong>Scleroderma complications can range from mild to life-threatening.</strong></p>

<p><strong>BLOOD CLOTS IN FINGERS</strong></p>

<p>Scleroderma can cause blood to clot in the fingers. Some burial insurance companies don’t care about blood clots in the fingers. If you have this condition, we will match you with the company that considers this condition as a non-issue.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option: </strong>Level death benefit plan with first-day coverage</p>

<p><br><strong>GASTROESOPHAGEAL REFLUX DISEASE (GERD)</strong></p>

<p>This can cause heartburn when the food leaks backward from the stomach to the esophagus. If this condition is not controlled, erosive esophagitis, esophageal bleeding, and Barrett’s esophagus may develop. This may lead you to become high-risk life insurance. Uncontrolled GERD will cause you to pay more and have a waiting period.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p><br><strong>AMPUTATION </strong></p>

<p>Scleroderma can damage the fingertips and toes, resulting in ulcers and gangrene that need to be amputated. If you have amputation from scleroderma, guaranteed issue burial insurance will be your only option.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p><br><strong>KIDNEY DAMAGE</strong></p>

<p>Scleroderma may cause high blood pressure, proteinuria, and kidney failure. If you have acute kidney failure from scleroderma, you will need to pay more for the first two years. Once the two years have passed, you can qualify for an immediate benefit. If you have chronic kidney failure, you must take a guaranteed life insurance.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p><br><strong>ORGAN TRANSPLANT&#160;</strong></p>

<p>Scleroderma may cause lung complications that may need a lung transplant. If you had been advised to have a lung transplant or an organ transplant, you would only qualify for guaranteed issue burial insurance.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> Guaranteed issue burial insurance</p>

<p><br><strong>ARRHYTHMIA &amp; CONGESTIVE HEART FAILURE</strong></p>

<p>These conditions may result from scarring of the heart tissue. If you have an <a href="/burial-insurance/arrhythmia/">arrhythmia</a> or irregular heartbeat, you can still qualify for immediate coverage. However, if you develop <a href="/burial-insurance/congestive-heart-failure/">CHF</a> because of scleroderma, you will only qualify for a first-day benefit plan.</p>

<p>You will be covered from the first day, and your death benefit will be phased in over time.</p>

<p><strong>Best Option:</strong> First-day benefit plan</p>

<h2 id="do-i-need-medical-exam-if-you-have-scleroderma"><br><strong>Do I Need A Medical Exam To Qualify For Burial Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>You are NOT required to take a medical exam to qualify for burial insurance with scleroderma.</p>

<p>When you apply for burial insurance, you only have to answer some basic questions about your health. The application process is simple; you don’t need to provide medical records or blood and urine samples.</p>

<p>You’ll get the official approval from the insurance company often within minutes!</p>

<h2 id="insurance-underwriting-for-scleroderma"><br><strong>Burial Insurance Underwriting If You Have Scleroderma</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Burial insurance companies have two ways of underwriting:</strong></p>

<p><strong>FIRST</strong> – They may ask you a series of health questions. Your answers to their questions will determine your eligibility.</p>

<p><strong>SECOND</strong> – They will electronically review your prescription history to verify your health.</p>

<p><br><strong>HEALTH QUESTIONS</strong></p>

<p>Most burial insurance companies don’t ask about scleroderma on their health questionnaire. Scleroderma on its own is a non-issue to most insurance companies if it is localized and does not affect major organs.</p>

<p><strong>If you see an insurance company asks about this condition, you will most likely see them ask about scleroderma this way:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Have you ever been diagnosed with or been treated for scleroderma?</li>
</ul>

<p>They rarely ask about scleroderma in the application. But, we will help you find the insurance company that does not ask about scleroderma.</p>

<p><br><strong>PRESCRIPTION HISTORY CHECK</strong></p>

<p>Burial insurance companies with underwriting also conduct prescription history checks to see what medications you take for scleroderma.</p>

<p><strong>Here’s a list of drugs used to <a href="https://www.webmd.com/arthritis/understanding-scleroderma-treatment" target="_blank" rel="noopener">treat scleroderma</a>&#160;and its complications:</strong></p>

<p><strong>Skin problem</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Corticosteroid creams (Hydrocortisone, Clobetasol, Betamethasone) these creams are not flagged for life insurance.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Joint or muscle pain</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory meds (NSAIDs) (Aspirin, Ibuprofen, and Naproxen)</li>
<li>Corticosteroid pills (Prednisone, Prednisolone, Methylprednisolone).</li>
<li>Taking any of these drugs will not affect your life insurance eligibility. These drugs are a non-issue with life insurance companies.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Raynaud’s phenomenon</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Vasodilators (Hydralazine, Minoxidil) <a href="/burial-insurance/high-blood-pressure/">High blood pressure</a> medications to treat scleroderma are okay with life insurance companies and won’t cause you to pay more premiums.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease or GERD</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Foaming agents (Gaviscon, Maalox, Mylanta)</li>
<li>H2 blockers (Tagamet, Pepcid, Zantac)</li>
<li>Proton pump inhibitors (Aciphex, Prevacid, Prilosec). If scleroderma causes GERD and you’re taking any of these medications, it will not affect how much you pay for life insurance.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Kidney problems</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor – Benazepril (Lotensin) Enalapril (Vasotec) Captopril.&#160; Kidney medications is a non-issue with most burial insurance companies. You will not be penalized for taking these medications.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Systemic Scleroderma</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Immunosuppressant (Mycophenolate, Cyclophosphamide, and Methotrexate). Immunosuppressant medications are used for treating scleroderma and other medical conditions like tissue or organ transplant. This may cause the life insurance agency to charge a higher premium and a waiting period.</li>
</ul>
