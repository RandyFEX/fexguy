<h1>Burial Insurance for Insulin Dependent Diabetics</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong><br>• Most insulin diabetics can still qualify for burial insurance<br>• Poor control or complications can raise rates or limit approval<br>• Guaranteed issue plans cost more and delay payouts for 2 years<br>• Many people assume denial when better options actually exist<br>• Waiting too long can push you into worse, more expensive coverage<br>• Not comparing companies can cost you thousands over time</p>

<p>Burial insurance for insulin dependent diabetics depends on how well your condition is managed. Insurance companies look closely at your A1C levels, complications, and overall health, not just the fact that you use insulin. If your diabetes is controlled, you may qualify for burial insurance, which is a type of whole life insurance, with first-day coverage and reasonable rates. If it’s unstable or has caused complications, you’ll likely be pushed into guaranteed issue plans with higher costs and a 2-year waiting period. The difference comes down to control, timing, and choosing the right company.</p>

<h2 id="best-option"><br><strong>What is my best insurance option if I’m an insulin-dependent diabetic?</strong></h2>

<p>If you’re managing your diabetes with insulin, you might just hit the jackpot with a level death benefit plan that offers full coverage from day one.</p>

<p>No waiting around, no fuss. And guess what? You might even snag the same sweet rates as someone who doesn’t have diabetes at all. How cool is that?</p>

<p><br><strong>OTHER FACTORS THAT MAY AFFECT ELIGIBILITY</strong></p>

<p><strong>NEUROPATHY</strong> (Nerve/Circulatory Issues due to diabetes)</p>

<p>Got some neuropathy? No worries. You can still score that top-notch level death benefit plan with first-day coverage with the right insurance company.</p>

<p><br><strong>NEPHROPATHY</strong> (Kidney issues due to diabetes)</p>

<p>Now, if you’re dealing with kidney issues and undergoing dialysis, burial insurance companies will take note. In this case, you can qualify for a first-day coverage plan if you apply to the right company and live in the zip code where this plan is available.</p>

<p><br><strong>RETINOPATHY</strong> (Eye issues due to diabetes)</p>

<p>Mild vision problems? You’re still golden. You’ll qualify for a level benefit plan, no problem.</p>

<p><br><strong>DIABETIC AMPUTATION</strong></p>

<p>If you’ve had an amputation due to diabetes, the insurance folks will want to know if you’re still rocking your daily routine independently. If you’re managing just fine and the amputation happened over two years ago, you might qualify for a first-day coverage plan.</p>

<p>But if you need help with daily living activities or had a recent amputation, then it’s time to look at guaranteed acceptance life insurance. Most burial insurance companies won’t offer first-day coverage if you need assistance with ADLs.</p>

<p><br><strong>INSULIN SHOCK</strong></p>

<p>Had a run-in with insulin shock? No problem, as long as it was more than two years ago. Most insurance companies will qualify you for the best coverage.</p>

<p>But if insulin shock happened in the last two years, our preferred life insurance company will ask” “Been in the hospital twice in the past two years? If your answer’s a ‘Nope,’ you’re lining up for a first-day coverage plan.</p>

<p>But if you’re currently in the hospital or have had a couple of visits in the last few years, your best bet is guaranteed-issue burial insurance with a two-year waiting period.</p>

<p><br><strong>DIABETIC COMA</strong></p>

<p>If your last diabetic coma was over two years ago, congratulations! Most life insurance companies still offer first-day coverage.</p>

<p>But if it happened within the last two years, you’ll qualify for a first-day coverage plan if you haven’t been hospitalized twice in the last couple of years. </p>

<p>If you’re currently in the hospital or have had a couple of visits in the last few years, your best option is guaranteed-issue burial insurance.</p>

<h2 id="underwriting"><br><strong><strong>Burial insurance underwriting for insulin-dependent diabetics</strong></strong></h2>

<p><strong>There are two ways these insurance folks figure out if you’re a good risk:</strong></p>

<p><strong>FIRST</strong> – They’ll grill you with a bunch of health questions.</p>

<p><strong>SECOND</strong> – They’ll dig through your medicine cabinet like a detective.</p>

<p><strong>HEALTH QUESTIONS</strong></p>

<p>Most burial insurance companies will ask you about diabetes and if you use insulin.</p>

<p><strong>Here are some questions you might see on the application:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Have you been diagnosed with diabetes prior to age 30 or have you ever been treated for insulin shock, diabetic coma, retinopathy, or diabetic neuropathy?</li>
<li>Have you ever been treated or diagnosed with insulin shock, diabetic coma, amputation caused by disease, or taken insulin shots prior to age 50?</li>
<li>Within the past 2 years have you had, or been diagnosed with, or received treatment or medication for complications of diabetes such as: diabetic coma, insulin shock, retinopathy, nephropathy, or neuropathy?</li>
</ul>

<p>These are the common questions you will encounter on the application.</p>

<p><br><strong>PRESCRIPTION HISTORY CHECK</strong></p>

<p>Insurance companies will look at your past medicines to see what health problems you’ve had. This is something most insurance companies do. </p>

<p>If you’ve taken medicine for any sickness, they can see it. If you have diabetes, they’ll look for certain medicines. If you’ve used these, they might think you need insulin.</p>

<p><strong>Here are some common diabetes medicines:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Aspart (Novolog)</li>
<li>Degludec (Tresiba)</li>
<li>Glargine (Basaglar)</li>
<li>Glulisine (Apidra)</li>
<li>Humulin</li>
<li>Insulin detemir (Levemir)</li>
<li>Lispro (Humalog)</li>
<li>Novolin</li>
<li>Novolog</li>
<li>Velosulin</li>
</ul>

<p>There might be other medicines, but these are the most common ones for people who use insulin.</p>
