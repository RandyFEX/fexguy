Burial insurance, which is a type of whole life insurance, is often the fallback if traditional policies decline you. The key is how long it’s been since the offense and whether there’s a pattern of repeat behavior, because that directly impacts your options and cost.</p>

<p id="UNDERSTANDING-BURIAL-INSURANCE-WITH-A-DUI-OR-DWI"><br><strong>UNDERSTANDING BURIAL INSURANCE WITH A DUI OR DWI</strong></p>

<p>DUI laws prohibit driving under the influence of alcoholic beverages, driving under the influence of drugs, and driving under the influence of any <a href="/burial-insurance/drug-abuse-treatment/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">drug</a> or <a href="/burial-insurance/marijuana-use/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">marijuana</a>, whether legal or illegal, regardless of blood-alcohol level.</p>

<p>Life insurance companies know that driving while intoxicated makes you more likely to die in a vehicle accident. If you already have a DUI, you are statistically more likely to drive under the influence again at a later time.</p>

<p><br><strong>Drunk driving charges can be referred to by the following acronyms:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>DUI – Driving Under the Influence</li>
<li>DWI – Driving While Intoxicated</li>
<li>OWI – Operating While Intoxicated</li>
<li>OVI – Operating a Vehicle While Intoxicated</li>
<li>OUI – Operating Under the Influence While Impaired</li>
</ul>

<p><br><strong>Other acronyms that refer to charges related to the severity of drunken driving charges include:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>ADWI – Aggravated Driving While Intoxicated</li>
<li>DWAI – Driving While Ability Impaired</li>
<li>DUII – Driving Under the Influence of Intoxicants</li>
<li>DUII-CS – Driving Under the Influence of Intoxicants: Controlled Substances</li>
<li>OMVI – Operating a Motor Vehicle While Impaired</li>
<li>OUII – Operating Under the Influence of intoxicating liquor</li>
</ul>

<p>DUI is the most common acronym used in most states for driving under the influence. States that use the DWI include Arkansas, Alaska, Connecticut, Louisiana, Missouri, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Nebraska, Texas, and West Virginia.</p>

<p><br><strong>The following acronyms are used in the states listed below:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>OUI – Massachusetts, Rhode Island</li>
<li>OVI – Ohio</li>
<li>OWI – Indiana, Iowa</li>
<li>DUII – Oregon</li>
</ul>

<p>Many burial insurance companies will ask you about DUI using any of these terms and deny your application if you have a DUI or DWI…but not all of them!</p>

<h2 id="insurance-underwriting-for-dui-or-dwi-history"><br><strong><strong>Burial Insurance Underwriting If You Have A DUI Or DWI History</strong></strong></h2>

<p>Underwriters assess your risk when you apply for life insurance coverage. They use all the data available to determine how likely or how soon you will die. They determine how much they will charge for your specific situation.</p>

<p>Every burial insurance company has unique underwriting guidelines. </p>

<p><strong>Burial insurance companies that have underwriting generally check:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Answer to health questions on the application</li>
<li>Medical history through the Medical Information Bureau</li>
<li>Medication history through Prescription database</li>
<li>Driving record through the Department of Motor Vehicle</li>
<li>Other companies may want to do a phone interview</li>
</ul>

<p>Your answer to the health questionnaire is the biggest factor in determining your eligibility.</p>

<p><br><strong>HOW DO INSURANCE COMPANIES FIND OUT ABOUT DUI?</strong> </p>

<p>Some life insurance companies that cover DUI customarily check the DMV records of applicants, so it’s unlikely you’ll be able to hide your DUI conviction from the insurer.</p>

<p>Any lies on the application are considered fraud, resulting in the insurance company rescinding your policy in the first two years if your lies are uncovered. Your beneficiaries would be denied payment if you die in the first two years if you lied on your application.</p>
