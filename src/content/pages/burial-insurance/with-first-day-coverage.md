<h2 id="benefits-of-first-day-coverage-insurance"><br><strong>Benefits Of Burial Insurance With First-day Coverage&#160;</strong></h2>

<p>Burial insurance with first-day coverage has many benefits, including:</p>

<p>1.&#160;<strong>Full insurance coverage starts upon first payment</strong>&#160;– your insurance coverage begins after you pay your first premium. Your beneficiary is entitled to receive 100% of your face amount when you pass away due to an accident or a medical reason.</p>

<p>2.&#160;<strong>Level premium</strong>&#160;– your monthly premium will remain the same. It will never increase due to your age or worsening health condition. You lock the price at the age you bought the policy so it will remain the same throughout your life.</p>

<p>3.&#160;<strong>Level benefit</strong>&#160;– your death benefit will never decrease. It is guaranteed to remain the same, and your beneficiary will receive the full death benefit when you pass away.</p>

<p>4.&#160;<strong>Lifetime coverage</strong>&#160;– you are fully covered your whole life. You can never outlive your policy because it will remain in force until age 121.</p>

<p>5.&#160;<strong>Non-cancelable</strong>&#160;– your policy cannot be canceled except for non-payment. Life insurance with first-day coverage will never be canceled due to age. Your policy will remain in force if you keep paying your premium on time.</p>

<p>6.&#160;<strong>Cash value grows over time</strong>&#160;– burial insurance with first-day coverage has a <a href="https://www.forbes.com/advisor/life-insurance/cash-value-life-insurance/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">cash value</a> that grows over time. You can withdraw your cash value or file a policy loan if you need funds.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-a-waiting-period"><br><strong>What Is A Waiting Period?</strong></h2>

<p>Most people who buy life insurance don’t realize that some insurance plans come with a two-year <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="/burial-insurance-with-no-waiting-period/" target="_blank">waiting period</a>… until they read the fine print!</p>

<p>Let’s look at a waiting period plan to understand the importance of first-day coverage better.</p>

<p>A waiting period (typically two years) is imposed by life insurance companies, where they will not pay the full death benefit if the insured dies due to the natural cause of death or health reasons.</p>

<p>Your beneficiary will only receive 100% death benefit if you survive the two-year waiting period.</p>

<p>The waiting period serves as a safety net for the insurance company. It is their way of reducing the risk of payout to those people who are terminally ill, intending to commit suicide, and those who will die shortly after purchasing their policy.</p>

<p>Suicide during the waiting period can cause disqualification regarding death benefit payout. The waiting period helps discourage people thinking about suicide from purchasing burial insurance.</p>

<p>The waiting period allows the insurance carriers to contest a claim filed during the first two years of the policy. It gives them enough time to conduct a proper investigation if the policyholder has been honest on the application.</p>

<p><strong>A waiting period works this way:</strong></p>

<p>If you die during the waiting period, your beneficiary will receive only the premiums you paid plus some interest, typically 7-10%.</p>

<p><strong>For example:</strong></p>

<p>If you bought a $25,000 guaranteed issue life insurance policy with a two-year waiting period. Your beneficiary will not receive the $25,000 death benefit payout. They will only get all your premiums for one year plus 10% interest.</p>

<p>You must live for the first two years so your beneficiary can receive your full death benefit when you pass away.</p>

<h2 id="benefit-periods-in-life-insurance"><br><strong>What Are The Different Death Benefit Periods In Life Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p><strong>1. NO WAITING PERIOD</strong></p>

<p>Your best death benefit option is burial insurance with first-day coverage and no waiting period. </p>

<p>Burial insurance companies that offer no waiting period policies will pay the full death benefit when you pass away due to an accident or natural cause of death.</p>

<p>Do you want to know the secret to tell if a company offers first-day coverage? </p>

<p>They ask health questions. Yes, you heard it right. The insurance companies ask health questions to offer you first-day coverage.</p>

<p>The good news is that most people qualify for burial insurance with first-day coverage and no waiting period…even when they are not in perfect health. </p>

<p><strong>2. PARTIAL WAITING PERIOD</strong></p>

<p>Burial insurance offering partial waiting period policies has first-day coverage but a phase-in death benefit schedule. It is also called a graded benefit plan.</p>

<p>Insurance policies with a partial waiting period don’t pay the full death benefit during the first two years. They will only pay a portion of the death benefit depending on the time you pass away.</p>

<p>The partial waiting period plan death benefit schedule looks like this:</p>

<ul>
<li>First 12 months – 30%-40% death benefit</li>
<li>13-24 months – 70%-80% death benefit</li>
<li>After 24 months – 100% death benefit</li>
</ul>

<p>Some health issues will not qualify for first-day coverage like:</p>

<ul>
<li>Alcohol or drug abuse treatment</li>
<li>Cancer within the last 24 months</li>
<li>Congestive heart failure</li>
<li>Diabetic amputation</li>
</ul>

<p>If you have any of these conditions, your best option for insurance coverage is a partial waiting period or a graded plan.</p>

<p><strong>3. TWO-YEAR WAITING PERIOD</strong></p>

<p>Guaranteed issue life insurance or guaranteed acceptance life insurance comes with a two-year waiting period.</p>

<p>Guaranteed acceptance life insurance only offers first-day coverage for the accidental cause of death. However, it has a two-year waiting period for the natural cause of death or health-related death,</p>

<p>If you have a guaranteed issue life insurance and die during the first two years, your beneficiary will only receive the premiums you paid plus 7-10% interest.</p>

<p>How can you tell if a life insurance policy has a two-year waiting period? Simple, if there are no health questions on the application. No questions asked life insurance automatically has a two-year waiting period.</p>

<p>The insurance company never asks health questions with a guaranteed issue life insurance policy. Your approval is guaranteed regardless of your medical condition.</p>

<p>If you’ve been diagnosed with a chronic illness, terminal illness, or confined in a care facility, life insurance with a waiting period is your only option for coverage.</p>

<h2 id="why-insurers-evaluate-your-health"><br><strong>Why Do Insurance Companies Evaluate Your Health Before Offering First-day Coverage Insurance?</strong></h2>

<p>Burial insurance companies offering first-day coverage insurance ask health questions.</p>

<p>Insurance companies are in the business of managing risk, and they will evaluate your general health before approving your coverage.</p>

<p>They face a tremendous amount of risk if they offer first-day coverage to people who are on their deathbeds. By evaluating your health, they are assured that they will stay in business in the future.</p>

<p>They want to ensure you will not die immediately and pay a huge death benefit payout.</p>

<p>Most insurance companies have lenient underwriting. Most health issues are offered first-day coverage. However, some health issues are considered a high risk that no insurance company is willing to offer first-day coverage.</p>

<p><strong>The following health issues will result in a two-year waiting period:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Alzheimer’s disease</li>
<li>Angina within the last 12 months</li>
<li>Recent cancer and cancer treatment</li>
<li>Confined to hospital, hospice care, or nursing home</li>
<li>Diabetic coma within the last 24 months</li>
<li>Have an organ transplant or advised to receive an organ transplant</li>
<li>Heart attack within the previous 12 months</li>
<li>Heart or circulatory surgery within the previous 12 months</li>
<li>HIV or AIDS</li>
<li>Oxygen Use except for sleep apnea</li>
<li>Renal failure, dialysis</li>
<li>Terminal illness</li>
</ul>
