<h2 id="h-why-colonial-penn-is-a-bad-deal-for-most-people"><strong>WHAT IS COLONIAL PENN’S $9.95 LIFE INSURANCE PLAN?</strong></h2>

<p>Colonial Penn sells guaranteed acceptance whole <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_insurance#Permanent_life_insurance" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">life insurance</a> using a unit system that gives very small coverage for each $9.95 payment.</p>

<p>The company has been around for decades and markets heavily to seniors through television commercials featuring familiar faces and comforting music.</p>

<p>The ad sounds simple because they offer life insurance for only $9.95 a month, guaranteed acceptance, and no health questions. But what they don’t emphasize is that $9.95 only buys <strong>one “unit”</strong> of coverage. It’s not a full policy. It’s only a partial policy for about 99.9% of seniors.</p>

<p>Each “unit” equals a small amount of death benefit, usually between $400 and $1,500, depending on your age and gender.</p>

<p>The older you are, the smaller your benefit per unit. You can buy multiple units, but each additional unit costs another $9.95!</p>

<p>For someone who needs $10,000 in protection, the real price could be $60 to $120 per month, not $9.95. Even over $200 a month is not uncommon.</p>

<p>It’s also important to know that this plan is guaranteed issue, which means the company doesn’t ask health questions. Everyone gets approved if they can afford the outlandish prices Colonial Penn charges.</p>

<p>Because Colonial Penn accepts everyone, it forces everyone into a two-year waiting period.</p>

<p>If you pass away from natural causes during the first two years, your family doesn’t get the full benefit. They only refund what you’ve paid so far, plus a small amount of interest.</p>

<p>This structure protects the company, not you as a customer. It’s the tradeoff you make when you buy guaranteed acceptance life insurance instead of qualifying for massively better first-day coverage.</p>

<p>I’ve reviewed hundreds of these policies for families who thought they were protected. They weren’t.</p>

<h2><strong>DOES COLONIAL PENN HAVE A WAITING PERIOD?</strong></h2>

<p>Colonial Penn delays full payouts for natural death during the first 2 years, refunding premiums instead of paying the benefit.</p>

<p>That means if you pass away from natural causes within the first 24 months, your loved ones will not receive the full death benefit. Instead, Colonial Penn refunds the premiums you’ve paid, plus a small amount of interest at 7% total.</p>

<p>That’s the tradeoff when a company promises “guaranteed acceptance.” They can’t ask about your health, so they protect their bottom line by delaying when your coverage actually starts.</p>

<p>During those two years, only accidental deaths, like a car accident or a fall, would pay the full benefit. Any other cause, including a heart attack, cancer, or stroke, would not.</p>

<p>This is the single most misunderstood part of the Colonial Penn $9.95 offer. The ads never clearly say, “There’s a two-year waiting period before your coverage pays out.” They focus on comforting words like “peace of mind” and “guaranteed acceptance,” which sound safe but hide a terribly 2-year wait plan.</p>

<p>Guaranteed-issue life insurance like Colonial Penn’s should never be an option that you would consider.</p>

<li>If guaranteed issue is your only choice, there are other companies with massively lower pricing and between 10-30% refund of premium interest rates.</li>
