<h1>Declined for Life Insurance – What To Do Now?</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong><br>• Getting declined doesn’t mean you can’t get life insurance elsewhere<br>• Every company underwrites differently, so approval varies widely<br>• Not knowing why you were declined leads to repeat denials<br>• Guaranteed issue plans cost more and limit early payouts<br>• Waiting and improving health can reopen better, cheaper options<br>• Applying blindly again can make your situation worse</p>

<p>Getting declined for life insurance feels like a dead end, but it’s not. The first step is to find out exactly why you were denied, because it could be due to health, lifestyle, or even incorrect information on your application. Once you know that, you can fix errors, improve your situation, or apply to a company that better handles your specific risk. Every insurer evaluates risk differently, so one denial doesn’t mean all insurers will say no. If a traditional term or whole life policy isn’t an option, burial insurance or guaranteed-issue whole life insurance can still provide coverage, though at a higher cost and with some limitations.</p>

<h2 id="different-outcomes-of-life-insurance-application"><br><strong>Different Outcomes Of Life Insurance Application</strong></h2>

<p><strong>After your life insurance application has gone through the life insurance company underwriting process, the life insurance company will decide on your approval in 1 of 4 ways:</strong></p>

<ol>
<li><strong> Approved as applied</strong> – this is the best application outcome. Your application is approved at the same amount of premium you were initially quoted.<br></li>
<li><strong> Approved other than applied</strong> – you are approved for coverage but at a different rate from what you applied for. The rate or premium amount could be better, but it is usually worse than expected. You will need to approve the new premium amount before they finally issue the policy.<br><br>There are many reasons why your approval decision is different than what was applied for. The result of your medical exam may cause you to be rated, or you may have neglected to give relevant information discovered during underwriting.<br></li>
<li> <strong>Postponed – </strong>typically happens if a disease diagnosis or treatment is very recent, and the insurer is unwilling to gamble on a medical issue reoccurring. Most insurance companies will tell you to apply again 6 months to 5 years after treatment. If your application is postponed, expect to get detailed information on how to get coverage in the future.<br></li>
<li><strong>Declined</strong> – this is the worst outcome. The insurance provider you applied for isn’t willing to give you any coverage. Read on to know why life insurance companies will decline your application.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="reasons-you-ll-get-declined-for-life-insurance"><br><strong>Reasons You’ll Get Declined For Life Insurance</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Here are the reasons why you would be declined for life insurance:</strong></p>

<p><strong>#1. An elevated level of Cholesterol, Lipids, and Triglycerides</strong></p>

<p>Your application for life insurance may be denied if you have elevated <a href="/burial-insurance/high-cholesterol/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">cholesterol</a> levels. In some cases, it may not be a reason for the decline, but being approved other than applied for is because you are at higher risk for heart disease and stroke.</p>

<p><strong>#2. Elevated Glucose or Blood Sugar Level</strong></p>

<p>High glucose or blood sugar level can indicate diabetes, which is a cause of the decline. To understand this, we have put a separate article on diabetes. If you have diabetes, please read our article <a href="/burial-insurance/diabetes/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Diabetic and Diabetes</a> burial insurance eligibility.</p>

<p>A high blood sugar level is often not discovered by applicants until they undergo a medical exam. High glucose or blood sugar levels usually have no noticeable physical symptoms.</p>

<p><strong>#3. Overweight or Obese</strong></p>

<p>Height to-weight ratio is one of the determining factors in getting approved at the best rate possible. Additionally, it also indicates your general health and fitness level.</p>

<p>Being overweight or obese may not always cause a decline; it usually results in higher premiums. But, a decline is most likely the outcome if it exists with other health risks.</p>

<p>Being overweight or obese is a red flag for life insurance companies because it can often lead to severe health complications, specifically cardiovascular issues.</p>

<p><strong>#4. Elevated Liver Function</strong></p>

<p>Life insurance companies may deny your application if they find out you have a high liver function. This condition can indicate inflammation or damage to the liver cells. It can be mild or temporary, but sometimes it can mean more severe liver problems.</p>

<p>If you have a mild liver function and are denied life insurance, try to reapply with another insurance carrier or get a new medical exam to get a better rate.</p>

<p><strong>#5. Blood or protein in the urine</strong></p>

<p>From the point of view of the insurance companies, the existence of blood or protein in your urine could indicate kidney disease. This is why life insurance companies would typically reject a life insurance application.</p>

<p><strong>#6. Hepatitis</strong></p>

<p>If you just discovered that you have any form of Hepatitis on the life insurance medical exam, it would mean a decline. However, Hepatitis B or C that has been diagnosed and treated will not cause you denial once your treatment is complete.</p>

<p>To get life insurance coverage, you must first begin treatment for Hepatitis. The treatment regimen can make you qualify for life insurance approval later.</p>

<p><strong>#7. A History of Cancer</strong></p>

<p>A history of <a href="/burial-insurance/cancer/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">cancer</a> puts you in a high-risk category in life insurance. Your life insurance approval or decline will depend on your cancer type and how long you have been in remission.</p>

<p>Skin cancer is viewed as a less severe form of cancer that life insurance companies typically approve. The more severe types of cancer, like breast cancer and lung cancer, may cause a decline.</p>

<p><strong>#8. HIV or AIDS</strong></p>

<p>Even though survival rates for <a href="/life-insurance-for-hiv-positive/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">HIV</a> or AIDS have improved over the years, some life insurance companies still consider them high-risk and may deny your application. We know of a few life insurance companies that look favorably to HIV patients, which are the companies you need to apply to.</p>

<p><strong>#9. Oxygen Use</strong></p>

<p>Every single life insurance company will inquire about oxygen use. There is only one scenario where you can secure life insurance using oxygen. That one exemption is sleep apnea. If you use oxygen for sleep apnea, your life insurance application will be declined.
