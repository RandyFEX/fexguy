<h2 id="Chapter-2:-What-is-Burial-Insurance---And-What-Isn’t-It?"><strong>Chapter 2: What is Burial Insurance – And What Isn’t It?</strong></h2>

<p>The first rule of Final Expense Life Insurance is to <strong>purchase the correct type of insurance policy</strong>, the first time!</p>

<p>Burial insurance, often called <strong>Final Expense Insurance</strong>, <strong>Cremation Insurance</strong>, or <strong>Funeral Insurance</strong>, is a small <strong>WHOLE-LIFE</strong> insurance policy marketed primarily to people aged 40-85 years old.</p>

<p>Burial insurance is a product offered by private companies that provide a wide range of approval options and pricing. It also builds cash value over time. It’s not an investment; it’s a valuable benefit used to cover final expenses.</p>

<p>The idea is that when you pass away, the policy pays out enough to cover your funeral, burial, or cremation expenses. Most policies offer coverage ranging from $5,000 to $25,000. Some companies offer higher amounts with higher premiums.</p>

<p><strong>TERM LIFE</strong> insurance is <strong>NOT ideal </strong>for burial, cremation, or final expenses.</p>

<p>You’ve likely seen ads from AARP, New York Life, Globe Life, or TruStage promising cheap term life insurance for seniors. These term life policies are referred to as “5-year renewable term” plans. That means the rate you start with only lasts for five years before the price increases again, and then does so in another five years.</p>

<p>Imagine you get a policy at age 60. You might start paying $40 a month. But by 65, it jumps to $65. By 70? Maybe $100. By the time you’re 75 or 80, you could be paying over $200/month for the same small amount of coverage…if you can afford it at all.</p>

<p>And when you stop paying, your term life premium won’t be refunded; <strong>your policy will be canceled and terminated.</strong></p>

<p>Term life plans will also automatically cancel beyond age 80, no matter how long you’ve paid into them. That means if you outlive the policy, your policy automatically cancels when your family needs it most.</p>

<p><strong>IULs (INDEXED UNIVERSAL LIFE)</strong> are total garbage policies when used for final expense needs. Any life insurance agent who proposes an IUL to you should be hung up on immediately.</p>

<ul>
<li>The #1 rule: <strong>Get the right policy the first time</strong> — final expense insurance should always be <strong>whole life.</strong></li>
<li>Burial insurance is a <strong>whole-life policy</strong> designed to cover funeral, burial, or cremation costs.</li>
<li>Avoid <strong>term life</strong> plans – they get more expensive every 5 years and <strong>cancel automatically by age 80.</strong></li>

<p><strong>FIRST-DAY COVERAGE</strong>, also called <strong>level benefit coverage</strong>, pays the full death benefit from the very <strong>FIRST DAY</strong>. This is the type of Final Expense Life Insurance you want to purchase because you’ll get the most coverage for the least amount of money.</p>

<p><strong>Whole life 1st-day coverage plans</strong> are available to people with a wide range of medical problems, so your health and medication are not your problem; they are my problem as the Final Expense Guy to solve, and that includes <strong>NO 2-year waiting period.</strong></p>

<p><strong>TWO-YEAR WAIT PLANS</strong> (called <strong>graded or guaranteed issue</strong> policies) delay full benefits until after the first two years (or 3 years with some even worse policies).</p>

<p>If a death occurs within the first two years due to a health or medical reason, your beneficiary will only receive a refund of premiums plus interest, <strong>not the full payout</strong>. These ONLY pay out in the first two years if you die in an <strong>accidental death</strong>.</p>

<p>Unfortunately, 2-year wait policies are often sold to people who qualify for 1st-day coverage because most call centers want to avoid the medical underwriting training needed to get you 1st-day coverage.</p>

<p>And because 2-year wait policies are more expensive,<strong> they get paid more</strong>, or they have to ask you to reduce the coverage you truly need. </p>

<p>It’s all happening behind the scenes…and most applicants don’t realize it.</p>

<p>This type of underwriting is known as <strong>simplified issue</strong>. It avoids lab work but still screens applicants based on their health. Although you may not need to visit a clinic, <strong>you’re health and medical history are still being evaluated.&#160;</strong></p>
