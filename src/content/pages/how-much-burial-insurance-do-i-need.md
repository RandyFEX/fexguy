<h2 id="what-is-burial-insurance"><strong>What is burial insurance? </strong></h2>

<p>Burial insurance is also called funeral insurance, cremation insurance, or final expense insurance, and it is meant to cover the cost of a funeral, burial, and other end-of-life expenses. After you pass away, buying burial insurance will not leave your family with a mountain of debt.</p>

<p>Most families don’t have the funds to pay for the funeral and burial. One study shows that approximately 62% of Americans have less than $1,000 in their savings account, which is insufficient to cover funeral expenses.</p>

<p>That’s the reason why final expense insurance is so important. Burial insurance can protect your family from a financial disaster upon your passing.</p>

<p>You can easily <a href="/the-importance-of-burial-insurance/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">qualify</a> for burial insurance because it has an easy underwriting process (easy health questions). Most people with pre-existing medical conditions qualify because there is no medical exam. </p>

<p>You only need to answer some health questions. Your application can be submitted online or over the phone. Approval is quick, with no need to wait a month or two to know if you are covered.</p>

<p>Burial insurance can be a great alternative for people who won’t qualify for traditional or whole life insurance because of health issues. You can qualify for burial plans regardless of a medical condition because there are plans that do not ask health questions.</p>

<p>Burial insurance has a wide range of coverage options. You can choose between $2,000 and $50,000, depending on the coverage you need. </p>

<p>Funeral insurance is whole life insurance that works this way:</p>

<ul>
<li>Fixed monthly premiums for life</li>
<li>The policy will never expire when you reach 80 years old</li>
<li>The death benefit amount is guaranteed never to decrease</li>
<li>Accumulates cash value</li>
</ul>

<p>If you do not expect to have enough funds to cover your burial and final expenses at the end of your life, then a burial insurance policy might make sense for you. </p>

<p>Even if you expect to leave enough money for your funeral budget, you may not want your estate to be depleted by your funeral expenses. Having a final expense insurance policy is one way to ensure that your entire estate will be available to your loved ones.</p>

<h2 id="types-of-burial-insurance"><br><strong>Types of burial insurance</strong> </h2>

<p><strong>SIMPLIFIED ISSUE BURIAL INSURANCE</strong></p>

<p>Simplified issue whole life insurance offers immediate coverage. This plan is burial insurance with <a href="/burial-insurance/life-insurance-with-no-waiting-period/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">no waiting period</a>. Coverage starts from the very first day! It does not require a medical exam, and the company underwrites your health based on your answer to several health questions.</p>

<p>Simplified issue insurance has a lower face amount or coverage than term insurance. </p>

<p>The underwriting is relaxed, but you can be denied coverage because of some pre-existing medical conditions, terminal illness, smoking, or taking part in risky activities or occupations.</p>

<p><strong>Possible questions you might see on the application include:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Have you been diagnosed with a terminal illness like cancer in the last two years?</li>
<li>Do you have HIV, AIDS, or AIDS-related complex?</li>
<li>Do you suffer from heart disease or a stroke?</li>
<li>Do you have kidney disease requiring dialysis?</li>
<li>Do you have any cognitive impairment like dementia or Alzheimer’s disease?</li>
<li>Do you have a disability that needs a wheelchair or confinement to a nursing home?</li>
<li>Do you need help with activities of daily living such as eating, bathing, dressing, toileting, continence, and transferring?</li>
</ul>

<p>Minor health issues like high cholesterol or high blood pressure are considered normal and non-issue. </p>

<p>The main point of health questions in simplified issue insurance is to rule out potentially high-risk applicants, like those with terminal illnesses like cancer.</p>

<p>Simplified issue burial insurance is the best option for people over 50 up to 85 years old, who may have some health issues that could prevent them from getting coverage through the traditional way. </p>

<p>This is an excellent option if you’re not in perfect health or are too busy to take a medical exam, or simply have a fear of needles.</p>

<p><br><strong>GUARANTEED ISSUE BURIAL INSURANCE</strong></p>

<p>If you are between 50-85, guaranteed issue whole life insurance will provide your family with up to $25,000 of coverage without a medical exam or health questions. Your approval is guaranteed!</p>

<p>A Guaranteed Issue (GI) policy provides insurance coverage regardless of your health, age, or any other risk factors. A GI policy is life insurance for the elderly or people who cannot qualify for any type of life insurance due to high-risk factors; almost anyone who can enter into a legal contract can be eligible.</p>

<p>Here are some examples of the most common medical conditions where a guaranteed issue burial life expense policy is your only option:</p>

<ul>
<li>Alzheimer’s or dementia</li>
<li>Dialysis</li>
<li>HIV or AIDS </li>
<li>Organ transplants</li>
<li>Oxygen use</li>
<li>Terminal illness</li>
<li>Wheelchair-bound because of chronic disease</li>
<li>Mobility issue, needing help with any activities of daily living</li>
</ul>

<p>Guaranteed issue policies will accept applicants with these medical issues. </p>

<p>Since insurance companies assume a higher risk in providing coverage, the cost of guaranteed acceptance policies is generally higher than other insurance policies.</p>

<p>Guaranteed issue life insurance has a graded benefit for the first two years. The two-year waiting period applies to the natural cause of death. If you pass away due to natural causes during the two-year waiting period, your beneficiary will receive only the premiums you paid plus 7-10% interest. </p>

<p>Accidental death is fully covered even during the two-year waiting period, and the life insurance company will pay your beneficiary 100% of the death benefit. After the waiting period, you are covered for life, and your beneficiary will receive a 100% death benefit if you die for any reason.</p>

<h2 id="how-much-does-a-funeral-cost"><br><strong>How much does a funeral cost? </strong></h2>

<p>Your <a href="/how-much-does-final-expense-insurance-cost/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">funeral cost</a> is the first thing that will come to your mind because it’s often the biggest single expense. Other “final expenses” to consider – outstanding medical bills, living expenses, legal costs, and credit card bills, to name a few.</p>

<p>According to the National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA) website, the average cost of an adult funeral today is $8,755. </p>

<p>This amount only includes the most commonly used items for a traditional funeral. It doesn’t include the cost of the cemetery. A burial plot can cost about $2,000, so the average funeral and burial cost could be over $10,000.</p>

<h2 id="guide-questions-to-calculate-your-final-expenses"><br><strong>Guide questions to calculate your final expenses</strong></h2>

<p>The amount of burial insurance you need will depend upon your final wishes. </p>

<p><strong>Use these guide questions to help you determine how much burial insurance you need:</strong></p>

<ol>
<li>Do you want to be embalmed?</li>
<li>Do you want to be cremated or buried?</li>
<li>What type of casket do you want?</li>
<li>Would you have a viewing? How many days?</li>
<li>What kind of headstone do you want?</li>
<li>How far is the funeral home from your house?</li>
<li>What kind of service will you use for transportation?</li>
<li>What type of funeral home facilities will your family need to use?</li>
<li>How many death certificates will your family need to close your estate?</li>
<li>How much allowance will you allot for other final expenses?</li>
</ol>

<p>You can request a general price list from your nearest funeral home to know how much each service costs. Add up all these funeral costs to determine how much burial insurance you need.</p>

<h2 id="how-much-burial-insurance-do-i-need"><br><strong>How much burial insurance do I need? </strong></h2>

<p>Once you understand the importance of buying burial insurance, you’ll want to figure out how much coverage you will need. </p>

<p><strong>You can do so by doing the following:</strong></p>

<ol>
<li>Determine how much insurance you need to cover your final expenses. </li>
<li>Compute the cost of everything you need for the funeral and burial to determine how much insurance coverage you need. You should do some online research to determine the funeral cost in your area.</li>
<li>Include other costs such as medical bills and any outstanding debts on the computation. Other end-of-life expenses you will need to consider include remaining medical bills, legal costs, living expenses, credit card bills, and other past-due accounts.</li>
<li>Factor the inflation in your computation. The average funeral and burial cost is around $10,000, and it’s wise to plan on at least $15,000 to cover inflation.</li>
</ol>

<p>Considering all these things will enable you to calculate
how much burial insurance coverage you should buy. </p>

<h2 id="burial-insurance-costs"><br><strong>Burial insurance costs </strong></h2>

<p>The average premium for seniors’ burial insurance is typical $50 per month, and the most common coverage is $10,000. You can pay your premium weekly, monthly, semi-annually, or annually. Some insurance companies give a discount if you pay your policy annually. </p>

<p><strong>Your monthly premium amount might be higher or lower depending on some factors such as:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Age</li>
<li>Gender</li>
<li>Face Amount</li>
<li>Tobacco usage</li>
<li>Health condition</li>
<li>Pre-existing condition</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>AGE</strong></p>

<p>Every life insurance company has a set price for every specific age. Some have a price for ages 50, 51, 52, and so on. While other companies set their price according to age band. For example, 50-55 have the same pricing, increasing when you reach 56.</p>

<p>If you buy burial insurance when you are 50, you’ll pay less than if you wait until you’re 80 before purchasing a policy. The older you are, the higher premium you’ll pay because you present a higher risk to the insurance companies. </p>

<p>Insurance providers charge higher premiums the older you
get, but you lock in the premium at the age when you bought the policy, and the
amount will remain that way for life.</p>

<p><strong>FACE AMOUNT</strong></p>

<p>The amount of coverage you need will also determine the amount you have to pay. </p>

<p>For example, your cost per $1,000 is $5.57 per month. If you want $10,000 in coverage, your total monthly premium would be $55.70 per month. The higher the face amount, the higher the premium will be.</p>

<p><br><strong>TOBACCO USAGE</strong></p>

<p>Each burial insurance company has a specific rate for tobacco and non-tobacco users. They have different premiums for each, and they usually charge higher to applicants who use tobacco.</p>

<p>Cigarette smoking is a major health risk that can significantly reduce life expectancy. This is the reason why tobacco users pay more for life insurance.</p>

<p>It is important to understand that some insurance companies
give a non-tobacco rating if you chew, smoke cigars, smoke a pipe, or smoke
e-cigarettes. Cigarette smoking has a tobacco rating.</p>

<p><br><strong>YOUR HEALTH AND PRE-EXISTING CONDITION</strong></p>

<p>Your health condition also factors in the cost of burial insurance. Your current health determines your risk classification. The riskier you are to insure, the more expensive the premium.</p>

<p>A pre-existing medical condition can cost you more because medical conditions can significantly affect your life expectancy. Some pre-existing conditions can be so high-risk that getting immediate coverage is impossible.</p>

<p>The best part about buying burial insurance in your 50s or younger is that you lock in a lower premium for the rest of your life. Being younger means, you are less likely to have any serious illness that could prevent you from qualifying for immediate coverage with no waiting period.</p>
