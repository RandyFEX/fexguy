# Lincoln Heritage Funeral Advantage Review – Worst Insurance EVER?

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong></p>

<p>• Lincoln Heritage Funeral Advantage is usually more expensive than competitors<br>• Its agents are captive, so you won't see better options from other companies<br>• A "yes" to any significant health question means a modified death benefit<br>• A higher premium can cost you thousands of dollars over the life of the policy<br>• The Funeral Consumer Guardian Society helps with funeral planning, but it doesn't pay any funeral costs<br>• Approved claims are paid quickly, but the 24-hour clock starts after approval, not at the death</p>

## Company history and ownership

Jack Londen founded Lincoln Heritage Life Insurance Company in 1963. Today it's part of Londen Insurance Group and is based in Phoenix, Arizona. It's legally based (domiciled) in Illinois.

Funeral Advantage is underwritten by Lincoln Heritage Life Insurance Company. The company sells it through its own agents, who meet with people in person or by phone. You can't buy a policy online. Funeral Advantage isn't available in New York or Washington.

### Financial strength

AM Best rates Lincoln Heritage A (Excellent) with a stable outlook. AM Best raised the rating from A- in July 2025 and confirmed it in August 2026.

That rating means Lincoln Heritage has the financial strength to pay claims. It's important to separate financial strength from consumer value. A strong rating tells you nothing about whether the price is fair.

## Is Funeral Advantage a government program?

No. You may see ads for the "Funeral Advantage program," sometimes next to phrases like "aids seniors" or "state-regulated." Funeral Advantage is just a whole life final expense policy. It's not a government program, a special state benefit, or regulated differently from any other private life insurance policy. Lincoln Heritage's own website says that neither the federal government nor any state agency endorses or is affiliated with the company.

Being "state regulated" only means the policy must follow the same rules as every other insurer. Our guide to [state-regulated life insurance](/burial-insurance/state-regulated-life-insurance/) explains these ads. The only federal funeral benefit is Social Security's one-time $255 death payment to an eligible spouse or child.

So when you see a Lincoln Heritage postcard or Facebook ad that looks like a government form, remember this: the marketing is private, the program is private, and the purpose is to sell you one specific policy from one specific company.

## How Lincoln Heritage underwriting decides your coverage

There's no medical exam. A Lincoln Heritage agent asks the application's health questions, and your answers decide whether you can get coverage and which plan you get. The application also authorizes Lincoln Heritage to get your prescription history from pharmacies and use it to evaluate your application.

The application sorts the health questions into two groups. A "yes" to an uninsurable condition means you can't get the policy. A "yes" to any significant health condition question means your death benefit will be modified.

| Result | If the applicant… |
|---|---|
| Uninsurable | Has tested positive for HIV, or has been diagnosed with AIDS or a life expectancy of 12 months or less |
| Uninsurable | Is currently bedridden, in a hospital or care facility, or receiving hospice care |
| Modified death benefit | In the past two years was diagnosed with, treated by a doctor for, or took medication for heart disease (such as a heart attack, heart surgery or congestive heart failure) or circulatory disease (such as a stroke, an aneurysm, or advice to have surgery to improve circulation) |
| Modified death benefit | In the past two years was diagnosed with, treated by a doctor for, or took medication for cancer (except basal cell skin cancer) or lung disease such as COPD or emphysema (asthma doesn't count) |
| Modified death benefit | In the past two years was diagnosed with, treated by a doctor for, or took medication for liver or kidney disease, or had an organ transplant; Alzheimer's, dementia, organic brain syndrome or ALS; or alcohol or drug abuse |
| Modified death benefit | In the past two years was diagnosed with, treated by a doctor for, or took medication for complications of diabetes, such as an amputation, diabetic coma, blindness or a kidney disorder |
| Modified death benefit | Has had, or been advised to have, a diagnostic test related to any condition above (other than HIV or AIDS) and hasn't received the results yet |
| May qualify for immediate coverage | Answers "no" to every question. That doesn't guarantee approval: Lincoln Heritage still reviews the application, including your prescription history, before it decides. |

This summarizes Lincoln Heritage's Funeral Advantage application (form ICC13FEAPPR). Questions can vary by state and change over time.

Three things stand out:

- **Most questions cover the past two years.** They ask whether you were diagnosed, treated by a doctor, or took medication for these conditions during that time. If you still take medication for an older condition on the list, the answer is "yes." This two-year look-back isn't the same thing as the modified plan's limited-benefit period.
- **Common conditions aren't on the list.** The application doesn't ask about high blood pressure, high cholesterol or diabetes without complications. Lincoln Heritage still checks your prescription history, so answer every question accurately.
- **One "yes" is enough.** A single "yes" to any significant health condition question means a modified death benefit.

## Waiting periods: immediate vs. modified coverage

**Immediate coverage:** pays the full benefit from day one. Lincoln Heritage says most applicants qualify for it based on their health answers.

**Modified coverage:** Lincoln Heritage says it provides limited benefits for a non-accidental death during the early policy years. If you die from natural causes during that time, your family doesn't get the full amount. Neither Lincoln Heritage's website disclosure nor its application says how long that period lasts or exactly what it pays. Those details are in the benefit schedule of the policy you're offered, so get the schedule in writing before you sign.

For some unfortunate applicants, full protection doesn't start for years. That's a terrible plan to buy if you truly qualify for first-day coverage.

A modified plan is different from guaranteed acceptance life insurance, which asks no health questions at all.

**Contestability is a separate rule.** Every Funeral Advantage policy, immediate or modified, has a two-year contestability period. During those two years, Lincoln Heritage can deny a claim if the application contained a material misstatement. Answer every health question accurately. Our guide to the [contestability period](/burial-insurance/contestability-period/) explains how this works.

The policy also has a suicide exclusion with a refund of premium during the first two years (one year in Colorado, Minnesota, Missouri and North Dakota).

## The Funeral Consumer Guardian Society (FCGS)

Lincoln Heritage includes membership in the [Funeral Consumer Guardian Society](https://funeraladvantage.com/consumer-resources/what-is-the-funeral-consumer-guardian-society/) with Funeral Advantage at no additional membership cost. A former funeral director founded it in 2000. Buying a Lincoln Heritage policy is the only way to join, and Lincoln Heritage is the only insurance company it works with.

**What it does:**

- You fill out a final wishes form ahead of time.
- After a death, your family calls a 24-hour line and gets a personal funeral advisor.
- The advisor compares prices at funeral homes and reviews the funeral contract.

Lincoln Heritage says the service saves families an average of $1,850 on a traditional funeral and up to $1,100 on a cremation. Those are the company's own figures, not an independent study.

**What it doesn't do:**

- It doesn't pay a dime toward the funeral.
- It doesn't guarantee lower prices.
- It has no legal or financial authority over funeral homes.

The insurance policy is what pays.

## Riders and add-ons

The application lists two optional riders: a child rider and an accidental death and dismemberment (AD&D) rider. Ask the agent what each rider covers in your state and how much it adds to your premium.

## Pricing and cost factors

Your premium depends on your age, sex, coverage amount, state, any riders, and whether you get the immediate or modified plan. Tobacco matters too: the application asks whether you've used any form of tobacco in the past 12 months. Premiums never increase.

In my experience, Lincoln Heritage is frequently substantially more expensive than competing first-day coverage. Its prices are often 30% to 60% higher than comparable first-day plans from Aetna, Family Benefit Life or Mutual of Omaha.

That difference adds up over the life of a policy. Here's a simple illustration. It isn't a claim that every Lincoln Heritage customer pays exactly $40 more; your own difference depends on your quotes.

| If you pay $40 more per month | Extra cost |
|---|---|
| 1 year | $480 |
| 5 years | $2,400 |
| 10 years | $4,800 |
| 15 years | $7,200 |

To compare fairly, write down these five things from any quote:

1. The coverage amount
2. The monthly premium
3. Whether it's immediate or modified coverage
4. What the policy pays for a natural death in the early policy years
5. The tobacco status used

Then compare the same coverage and the same type of plan from several companies: first-day coverage against first-day coverage.

## How claims, cancellations and replacements work

**Claims:** Lincoln Heritage advertises that approved claims are paid within 24 hours. The clock starts after the claim is approved, not when the death happens. The beneficiary first sends the required documents, such as a certified death certificate. If the death happens during the two-year contestability period, the company can review the claim against the application before it approves it.

**Canceling:** contact Lincoln Heritage in writing and ask for written confirmation. The policy builds a very small cash value slowly, so canceling in the early years returns little or nothing.

**Replacing:** make sure the new policy is approved and active before you cancel the old one. A new policy starts its own contestability period, and if your health has changed, you may not qualify for the same coverage again.

## Common misunderstandings and sales tactics

- **"No medical exam" doesn't mean full coverage.** Your health answers can still put you in the modified plan, and Lincoln Heritage checks your prescription history.
- **"Claims paid in 24 hours"** means 24 hours after approval, and approval can take longer during the contestability period.
- **A no-cost funeral planning membership doesn't make a higher premium worth it.** Compare the price of the insurance itself.
- **Government-style ads** for a "program" or "state benefit" are private marketing for one company's policy.

## Better alternatives to Lincoln Heritage Funeral Advantage

The best alternatives are simplified issue whole life policies from highly rated companies that offer immediate first-day protection. These carriers use short health questionnaires to decide eligibility but don't require medical exams.

Competitors like [Aetna](/aetna-burial-insurance-review/), [Trinity Life](/trinity-life-insurance-review/) and [Family Benefit Life](/family-benefit-life-burial-insurance-review/) routinely approve applicants for first-day coverage, and Mutual of Omaha is worth comparing too. These insurers offer the same lifetime coverage that Lincoln Heritage promises, without the inflated premiums or waiting periods. Which one is best depends on your age, health and state.

Immediate coverage goes to people whose health fits Lincoln Heritage's narrow health underwriting guidelines. If you have health issues they don't like, you get the modified plan. The problem is that Lincoln Heritage may sell this plan to people who could have qualified for first-day coverage elsewhere, and it won't tell you about a better policy with better pricing.

Lincoln Heritage includes the Funeral Consumer Guardian Society at no additional membership cost. But if you're paying $40 more per month for the insurance, that's another $2,400 over five years. You have to ask yourself whether the funeral-planning service is worth paying that much more for the policy when comparable coverage may cost substantially less elsewhere.

Lincoln Heritage has the financial strength to pay claims, but its business model profits most when buyers don't compare pricing and features with other insurance companies. The company benefits from your loyalty. Your family benefits from your research and common sense.
