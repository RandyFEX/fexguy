The problem is that if you don’t qualify for that top tier, you get offered its Modified Plan with delays. Compared to other burial insurance options, Aflac sits in the middle. It’s solid, but not the cheapest or most flexible choice.</p>

<h2 id="how-it-works"><strong>What to ask before buying Aflac life insurance</strong></h2>

<h3><strong>Does Aflac offer first-day coverage?</strong></h3>

<p>Aflac has a burial insurance plan that offers first-day coverage.</p>

<p>If you qualify, their Whole Life Insurance Level Benefit Plan comes with immediate first-day coverage. You will be 100% covered from the first day, and your beneficiary will receive your full death benefit when you pass away.</p>

<h3 id="h-what-are-my-policy-options-with-aetna"><strong>What are my policy options with Aflac?</strong></h3>

<p>Aflac offers two final expense plans: a Level Plan and a Modified Plan.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Level benefit</strong>: first-day immediate coverage</li>
<li><strong>Modified benefit</strong>: two-year waiting period</li>
</ol>

<p>Your age and current health status will determine which plan you will be eligible for and the amount you will pay.</p>

<p><strong>#1</strong>: <strong>LEVEL BENEFIT PLAN:</strong></p>

<p>Issue ages: 45 to 80, age last birthday</p>

<ul>
<li>45-55 death benefit: $2,000-$50,000</li>
<li>56-65 death benefit: $5,000-$40,000</li>
<li>66-75 death benefit: $5,000-$30,000</li>
<li>76-80 death benefit: $5,000-$20,000</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>The level benefit plan is designed for healthier folks, but it accepts these health issues:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>High blood pressure</li>
<li>High cholesterol</li>
<li>Controlled diabetes</li>
<li>Arthritis</li>
<li>Mental health issues (bipolar, anxiety disorder, depression)</li>
<li>And they have a two-year lookback period on full stroke, TIA, heart attack, and circulatory surgeries.</li>
</ul>

<p>Aflac’s level benefit plans are generally competitively priced.</p>

<p><strong>#2</strong>: <strong>MODIFIED BENEFIT PLAN </strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Issue ages: 45 to 75, age last birthday</li>
<li>Minimum death benefit amount: $2,000</li>
<li>Maximum death benefit amount: $25,000</li>
<li>Death Benefits Schedule:</li>
</ul>

<p>Accidental death: 100% benefit immediately</p>

<p><strong>Non-accidental death:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>First 24 Months: 110% of paid premium</li>
<li>After 24 Months: Full death benefit</li>
</ul>

<p>The modified benefit plan has a full two-year waiting period. If you pass away during the first two years, they will pay your beneficiary the premium you paid plus 10% interest. Only in the third year will Aflac pay your full death benefit amount.</p>

<p>Aflac’s modified benefit plan is expensive compared to a guaranteed issue, no health questions policy. GI policy costs less, and it will approve your application regardless of the severity of your medical condition.</p>

<h3><strong>How is Aflac final expense whole life insurance good for seniors?</strong></h3>

<p>Aflac’s three final expense whole life insurance plans help ease the financial burden. However, among the three plans, the most beneficial is the level-benefit plan. This plan comes with first-day coverage, and the pricing is competitive. The graded plan is best for some health issues.</p>

<p>We do not recommend the modified plan for seniors because it has a two-year waiting period. Seniors will be better off with a guaranteed issue life insurance compared with this plan. A guaranteed issue policy will accept all applicants regardless of their medical condition.</p>

<h3><strong>Does Aflac have any hidden “Fine Print” in their policy?</strong></h3>

<p>You must be aware of the death benefit schedule when considering Aflac’s graded and modified benefit plans. I don’t recommend these plans, as most people will qualify for Aflac’s 1st-day coverage plan.</p>

<p><strong>Modified Death Benefit</strong></p>

<p>Non-accidental death</p>

<ul>
<li>Policy years 1 and 2: ROP plus 10% </li>
<li>Policy year 3: full death benefit</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="getting-approved"><strong>How do I get approved by Aflac?</strong></h2>

<p><strong>To qualify for burial insurance, ACI will check your health in three ways:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>They will ask health questions in the application</li>
<li>They will electronically check your prescription history</li>
<li>They will perform a telephone interview</li>
</ul>

<p>They do this to verify your health and assess your level of risk.</p>

<p>If you answer no to all the health questions in the application, you will qualify for a level death benefit plan. This plan has first-day full coverage and pays 100% death benefit when you pass away.</p>

<h2 id="when-it-makes-sense"><strong>When does Aflac burial insurance make the most sense?</strong></h2>

<p>They don’t have height and weight restrictions, and those on the heavier side will qualify for coverage.</p>

<p>Avoid their modified benefit plan; it’s a poor option because you can often get a better-priced policy with other insurance companies. If you are considering purchasing burial insurance from Aflac or other companies, comparing plans and rates is best before choosing the one that fits your needs.</p>

<p><strong>Aflac’s</strong>&#160;<strong>final expense plans accept the following health conditions, which other burial insurance companies typically decline:</strong></p>

<ol>
<li><strong>COPD</strong>: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease patients who do not smoke or use supplementary oxygen in the last two years will qualify for a graded benefit plan. Without treatment for more than 24 months, you will be eligible for level death benefit without a waiting period.</li>
<li><strong>Diabetes</strong>: diagnosed after 40 years old and does not use 40 units of insulin daily may qualify for the level death benefit.</li>
<li><strong>Heart attack</strong>: after 24 months, will qualify for the graded death benefit. A heart attack over 24 months ago qualifies for the level death benefit.</li>
<li><strong>Congestive heart failure:&#160;</strong>will qualify for the graded death benefit at any time.</li>
<li><strong>Cancer:&#160;</strong>diagnosis, and treatment within 24 months will qualify for a graded benefit plan.</li>
</ol>
