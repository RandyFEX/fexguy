<h2 id="what-is-kidney-failure"><strong>What is kidney failure?</strong></h2>

<p>Your kidneys, those two bean-shaped buddies in your back, are like the workhorses of your body. They filter waste products out of your blood, like a trusty colander. They also keep your fluids balanced, like making sure your favorite drink isn’t too watery.</p>

<p>But sometimes, these champs get tired and can’t keep up. That’s kidney failure, or end-stage renal disease (ESRD) as the doctors call it.</p>

<p><strong>Kidney functions:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Filtering waste products and toxins from your blood</li>
<li>Maintaining a healthy balance of fluids and electrolytes in your body</li>
<li>Producing hormones that regulate blood pressure and red blood cell production</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>There are two main ways your kidneys can slow down:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Acute kidney failure:</strong>&#160;This hits you suddenly, like when you forget your grocery list. It can happen from illness or injury, but with treatment, your kidneys might perk back up.</li>
<li><strong>Chronic kidney disease (CKD):</strong>&#160;This one’s more gradual, like a slow drip in the faucet. It builds up over time, often from conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure. Here, the damage is usually more permanent.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>There are ways to help your kidneys keep going:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Dialysis:</strong>&#160;This is basically an artificial kidney machine that cleans your blood. There are two options: hemodialysis (think getting your blood filtered at a clinic) and peritoneal dialysis (done at home, like self-care for your kidneys).</li>
<li><strong>Kidney transplant:</strong>&#160;This is where a kind stranger or family member gives you one of their healthy kidneys.</li>
</ul>

<p>If you’re worried about your kidneys, see a doctor. Catching problems early is important! And with a little care, your kidneys might keep on filtering for many years to come.</p>

That way, you can focus on your health without worrying about your burial or cremation expenses.</p>

<p>Prices can vary depending on your age, health, and the amount of coverage you want.</p>

<h2 id="best-insurance-option"><strong>What is my best insurance option if I have kidney failure?</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Chronic Kidney Disease (Renal Failure)&#160;</strong><br>Some companies offer “day one” coverage, meaning you’re insured right away, depending on your zip code. No medical exam, but answer some health questions to get the best rates. Coverage starts when you pay your first premium and lasts your whole life.</p>

<p><strong>Kidney Failure with Dialysis</strong></p>

<p>If you follow your doctor’s treatment plan, you can often still get “day one” coverage. But if you refuse dialysis, your only option is a guaranteed policy with a 2-year wait.</p>

<p><strong>Kidney Transplant</strong></p>

<p>You may qualify for 1st-day coverage if your transplant occured 5 or more years ago (if you go with the right company).</p>

<p>The only other option here is a no-exam, no-questions policy with a 2-year wait. Basically, these policies won’t pay out if you die within 2 years of getting them, but they’ll give you your money back with a little interest.</p>

<h2 id="burial-insurance-underwriting"><strong>Burial insurance underwriting with kidney failure</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Some common kidney problems or procedures the insurance company may ask about:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>chronic kidney disease</li>
<li>dialysis</li>
<li>end-stage kidney failure</li>
<li>kidney disease</li>
<li>kidney failure</li>
<li>kidney transplant</li>
<li>renal disease</li>
</ul>

<p>Be honest! If they ask about kidney problems, answer “yes” if you have them.</p>

<p><strong>You will often see kidney disease questions asked this way:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>During the past 24 months, have you been treated for kidney failure or chronic kidney disease?</li>
<li>Have you ever had, or been diagnosed with, or received or been advised to receive treatment or medication for kidney disease?</li>
<li>Within the past 12 months, have you been advised to have kidney dialysis?</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq"><strong>Frequently asked questions</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Can I increase the coverage amount for burial insurance if my kidney failure worsens over time?</strong></p>

<p>Yes, some plans let you increase coverage if your kidney condition worsens. But there might be rules, so check with your insurer first.<br></p>

<p><strong>Are there waiting periods associated with burial insurance for kidney failure, and how do they work?</strong></p>

<p>Yes, some plans, especially for folks needing a transplant, have waiting periods. During this time, you might not get the full payout, but your beneficiary would get your premiums back.<br></p>

<p><strong>Can I purchase burial insurance for a family member with kidney failure?</strong></p>

<p>Yes, absolutely! But your family member needs to be on board with it.<br></p>

<p><strong>Do burial insurance policies cover expenses beyond funeral costs for individuals with kidney failure?</strong><br>Yep! While burial insurance is meant for final expenses, your beneficiary can use the money however they see fit.<br></p>

<p><strong>If I’ve had a kidney transplant, does that impact my eligibility for burial insurance?</strong></p>

<p>You’ll likely qualify for a plan, but there’s often a two-year waiting period unless 5 years have passed and you must live in an acceptable correct zip code.</p>
