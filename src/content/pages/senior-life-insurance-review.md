<h1>Senior Life Insurance Company Review: Bad For Seniors?</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:</strong></p>

<p>• Senior Life insurance is easy to get, but often not the best value<br>• No exam sounds good, but usually means higher premiums<br>• Many policies include waiting periods with reduced early payouts<br>• Coverage amounts are small and may not cover full funeral costs<br>• Customer reviews are mixed, with frequent complaints about service<br>• You’re locked into one company instead of comparing better options</p>

<p>Senior Life Insurance is a final-expense whole-life insurance company that offers easy approval, even if you have health issues. That’s the main selling point. You can often qualify without a medical exam, which helps people who’ve been declined elsewhere. The tradeoff is cost and limitations. Coverage is usually smaller, and many plans include waiting periods before full benefits are paid. Reviews are mixed, with some complaints about service and claims handling. If you don’t compare options, you can end up paying more for less coverage.</p>

<h2 id="h-senior-life-insurance-pricing-and-value"><strong><strong>SENIOR LIFE INSURANCE</strong></strong> <strong>PRICING AND VALUE</strong></h2>

<p>Senior Life Insurance pricing typically costs more per dollar of coverage than comparable final expense policies from independent insurers like the Final Expense Guy.</p>

<p>Pricing is one of the biggest concerns raised by seniors. Many of them say Senior Life’s premiums were higher than they expected and much higher than those offered by companies through the Final Expense Guy.</p>

<p><strong>Possible reasons for these concerns:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>No-exam policies typically have higher costs because the life insurance company is assuming more risk</li>
<li>Their products are typically sold over the phone, which incurs additional costs for call centers, technology, commissions, and other expenses.</li>
<li>The amounts of their policies often have lower coverage limits that feel expensive relative to the coverage they provide.</li>
<li>Some seniors report that they pay far more in premiums than what will be delivered to their family upon death. For example, let’s say a senior pays $90 per month for 15 years; that is a total of more than $16,200 for a $10,000 payout policy.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="h-senior-life-insurance-legacy-assurance-explained"><strong><strong><strong>SENIOR LIFE INSURANCE</strong></strong></strong> <strong>LEGACY ASSURANCE EXPLAINED</strong></h2>

<p>Legacy Assurance is an add-on membership with planning perks that’s separate from the actual life insurance payout.</p>

<p>In fact, Legacy Assurance is a membership service offered in conjunction with Senior Life’s life insurance policies. Members (that’s you!) pay the Life or Legacy service fees in addition to their insurance premiums.</p>

<p><strong>Legacy Assurance promises additional services to members, such as:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Member discounts on caskets, urns, and other funeral merchandise</li>
<li>Assistance with planning and negotiating the costs with funeral homes</li>
<li>Continued support services for family members after the death</li>
</ul>

<p>Legacy Assurance is not <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_insurance#Permanent_life_insurance" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">life insurance</a>, as it provides no death benefit to your family.</p>

<p>Instead, it behaves like a consumer discount club. Many people find it useful; however, others were surprised to see they are paying for something extra that wasn’t actually part of their coverage.</p>

<p>This confusion is part of the reason we encourage a policy review with the <a href="/licenses/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Final Expense Guy</a>, given the differences between an actual life insurance policy and any value-added services sold as a membership service alongside the actual life insurance policy.</p>
