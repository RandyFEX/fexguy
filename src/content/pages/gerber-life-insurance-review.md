<h1>Gerber Guaranteed Life Insurance – This Policy Sucks!</h1>

<p><strong>Here’s the Bottom Line:<br>• </strong>Gerber Life is easy to get, but often costs more overall<br>• Guaranteed issue plans come with a strict 2-year waiting period<br>• Coverage amounts are smaller than those of many competing companies<br>• Most policies focus on whole life, not flexible options<br>• Many people choose it for the brand, not the best value</p>

<p>Gerber Life Insurance is known for simple approval and strong brand recognition, but that doesn’t mean it’s the best deal. Gerber Life Insurance offers term life, whole life, and guaranteed issue policies, mostly focused on smaller coverage amounts and easy qualification. The trade-off is cost and limitations. Guaranteed issue plans come with a 2-year waiting period before full benefits pay out, and pricing is often higher than other companies for similar coverage. If you don’t compare options, you can end up paying more for less protection.</p>

<h2 id="h-what-is-guaranteed-issue-final-expense-insurance"><strong>WHAT IS GUARANTEED ISSUE FINAL EXPENSE INSURANCE</strong></h2>

<p>Guaranteed Issue means exactly what it sounds like. If you are between 50 and 80 years old, Gerber will sell you a policy regardless of your health…guaranteed!</p>

<p><strong>Here’s what’s included:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Mandatory 2-year waiting period…even if you’re healthy</li>
<li>No health questions</li>
<li>No medical exams</li>
<li>Guaranteed approval</li>
</ul>

<p>The coverage amounts range from $5,000 to $25,000. Because it is whole life insurance, as long as you want to keep paying, the policy will last forever.</p>

<p>Your premiums stay the same every month, and the policy will even build a small cash value.</p>

<p>Seniors typically buy this policy so they can take care of funeral, burial, cremation, or final expense costs.</p>

<p>People like the idea that they won’t be leaving behind a surprise bill when they pass. However, there are some things every buyer should be aware of.</p>

<p><strong>Better: </strong>The Final Expense Guy can qualify you for 1st-day coverage at significantly lower rates than Gerber. We NEVER recommend Gerber if you qualify for 1st-day coverage.</p>

<h2 id="h-the-gerber-life-two-year-waiting-period"><strong>THE GERBER LIFE TWO-YEAR WAITING PERIOD</strong></h2>

<p>The horrible feature about Gerber Life Guaranteed Issue is the graded death benefit. It won’t pay out a penny if you die of a health or medical reason in the first two years.</p>

<p>Instead, they simply refund your premiums paid plus an extra 10 percent interest. The only exception is accidental death, which is payable from day one.</p>

<p><strong>Here is their exact verbiage:</strong></p>

<p><strong>“Our payment of a Graded Death Benefit for the first two (2) years of coverage enables Gerber Life to guarantee acceptance of all applicants ages 50 to 80. If death occurs within the first two policy years for any reason other than an accident, all premiums plus 10% interest shall be paid to your beneficiary.”</strong></p>

<p><strong>This will be the death benefit payout schedule with Gerber guaranteed acceptance insurance:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>First Year – Premiums Refunded + 10%</li>
<li>Second Year – Premiums Refunded + 10%</li>
<li>Third Year and Beyond – 100% death benefit</li>
</ul>

<p>This means that if a senior buys a $15,000 policy and dies from cancer in the first or second year, the family wouldn’t get anything to help defray burial, cremation, or funeral costs. They would merely get back what they had paid plus interest.</p>

<p><strong>This policy sucks,</strong> and seniors often complain about not being clearly advised about this aspect of the policy-sign-up process.</p>

<p>Working with a broker, such as The Final Expense Guy, often results in first-day coverage for seniors, even with significant health issues. If you purchased Gerber in the last one to five years, you could still qualify for a plan that offers first-day pay.</p>

<p>Gerber’s Guaranteed Issue premiums are significantly higher than those of most other insurance companies.</p>

<p>Because there are no health questions or qualification criteria, Gerber Life spreads the risk among all applicants. This significantly increases the price compared to standard 1st-day coverage plans.</p>

<p>For example, a 65-year-old woman buying $10,000 of coverage might pay about $65 per month. A man of the same age would pay close to $85. These are estimates, but they line up with the reports from many customers. If they increase the coverage amount to $25,000, the monthly cost rises considerably to more than $200.</p>

<p>With Gerber, seniors are often confused and complain about the cost once they see how little the amount of coverage is for all of the premiums that they are paying. </p>
