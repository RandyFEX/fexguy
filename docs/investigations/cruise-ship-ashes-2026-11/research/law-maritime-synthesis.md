# Scattering ashes from cruise ships: legal synthesis

Checked 2026-11-21. Entry IDs refer to `law-maritime.json`.

## United States (confirmed in regulation text)

1. **The general permit.** 40 CFR 229.1 is a general permit, so no application is needed (US-01). It covers:
   - anyone transporting human remains *from the United States* for burial at sea;
   - US-flagged or US-registered vessels and aircraft *from any location*.
2. **The 3-mile rule.** Cremated remains may be placed at any depth, but "no closer than 3 nautical miles from land" (229.1(a)(3)). "Land" is the territorial-sea baseline nearest the site, so the distance is not measured from the beach (US-02).
3. **Flowers and wreaths** must be "readily decomposable in the marine environment" (229.1(c)) (US-03).
4. **Reporting.** Every burial "shall be reported within 30 days" to the EPA Region the vessel departed from (229.1(d)) (US-04).
   - EPA's online Burial at Sea Reporting Tool (reportedly https://burialatsea.epa.gov/) is the usual way to report. It is known only from a search summary (US-05).
5. **Where US law reaches** (40 CFR 220.1(a) and 220.2) (US-07). The rules cover:
   - material transported from the US;
   - any US-flagged vessel, wherever it is;
   - dumping in the US territorial sea by anyone, including foreign ships carrying material loaded abroad.

   "Ocean waters" start at the baseline. Bays and rivers landward of it are outside the general permit. EPA guidance reportedly sends those questions to the states and the Clean Water Act, but that wording is unverified (US-08).

   In practice:
   - A foreign-flagged ship that leaves a US port with ashes aboard is covered by the general permit.
   - A foreign-flagged ship that loaded the ashes abroad and scatters them more than 12 nm offshore is not.
6. **National parks, including Glacier Bay.** 36 CFR 2.62(b) prohibits scattering ashes in National Park System areas "except pursuant to the terms and conditions of a permit, or in designated areas" (US-09).
   - No Glacier Bay-specific ash page or compendium entry could be read.
   - Glacier Bay's vessel rules (36 CFR part 13) say nothing about ashes.
7. **National marine sanctuaries** (US-11 to US-15):
   - **Monterey Bay** bans any discharge or deposit from a cruise ship except cooling water, exhaust, clean bilge water and anchor wash (15 CFR 922.132(a)(2)(ii)).
   - **Greater Farallones** and **Channel Islands** have similar discharge bans. None of the three lists cremated remains as an exception.
   - **Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale** sanctuary bans discharges only when they lack a required permit (922.184). A burial that complies with the EPA permit therefore appears to be allowed there. This is a reading of the text, not an agency statement.
   - **Permits.** NOAA can issue special use permits only for categories it has noticed in the Federal Register (922.31). NOAA materials reportedly list disposal of cremated remains as one of those categories (partially confirmed).
8. **Alaska and Hawaii state law** was not checked (US-16, US-17).

## International framework (unverified this session)

- **UNCLOS** (INT-01 to INT-03):
  - A coastal state's territorial sea reaches 12 nm and its contiguous zone 24 nm.
  - On the high seas, the flag state has exclusive jurisdiction (Art. 92).
  - Dumping in another state's territorial sea or EEZ needs that state's express prior approval (Art. 210(5)).
- **London Convention and Protocol** (INT-04):
  - No IMO statement was found on whether cremated remains count as "dumping".
  - The US treats them as ocean dumping covered by its general permit. Other states reportedly exempt ash scattering from licensing.
- **MARPOL Annex V** (INT-05): it has not been established whether scattering ashes counts as "garbage" under it. Don't say MARPOL allows or forbids it.

## Other countries

- **United Kingdom:** GOV.UK says permission is required for a burial at sea in the UK marine area (https://www.gov.uk/burial-at-sea). Whether scattering ashes is exempt from licensing was not confirmed (UK-01).
- **Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Norway, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Greece, Mexico, the Bahamas, the Caribbean, Japan and Singapore:** all unverified leads (AU-01 to SG-01). Each needs its official source read before publication.

## How the rules fit together with cruise-line policy (SYN-01)

1. **A cruise line's permission is a private policy, not legal authorization.** It cannot override:
   - the coastal state's law inside its 12 nm territorial sea;
   - the flag state's law;
   - US law when the ashes were loaded at a US port;
   - the rules of a protected area.
2. **Which law applies on a given cruise:**
   - **Ashes loaded at a US port:** the EPA rules apply. That means at least 3 nm from the baseline, decomposable flowers only, and a report to EPA within 30 days. In US parks and sanctuaries, NPS and NOAA rules also apply.
   - **Inside another country's 12 nm:** that country's rules apply.
   - **On the high seas:** the flag state's rules apply. These were not found for the common cruise flags.
3. **What a safe reader-facing statement looks like:**
   - Follow the US rules for any cruise from a US port.
   - Scatter only outside 12 nm unless the coastal state's rules are known.
   - Avoid marine sanctuaries and park waters unless there is a permit.
   - Report to EPA within 30 days.
