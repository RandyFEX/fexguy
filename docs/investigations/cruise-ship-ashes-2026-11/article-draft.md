# Scattering Ashes From a Cruise Ship: Cruise Line Policies, Rules and Planning Guide

## Quick answer

Yes, many cruise lines will help you commit a loved one's ashes to the sea,
but only with their permission and on their terms. Royal Caribbean,
Princess, Virgin Voyages, Fred. Olsen and Marella publish a written process.
Ambassador says it may be possible case by case, and Crystal says it does
not offer burials at sea from the ship. Several large lines, including
Norwegian, Celebrity, Holland America, Disney and Viking, publish nothing we
could find, so ask them in writing. Plan on a biodegradable urn, copies of
the death and cremation certificates, a request made before you sail, and a
ceremony held well offshore, often 12 nautical miles out.

Key points:

- Never scatter ashes from your balcony or a public deck on your own. Ask
  the cruise line first; the ship arranges the time and place.
- Most published policies require a biodegradable container, and several
  require the whole urn to go into the sea rather than loose ashes.
- US federal rules require cremated remains to be placed at least 3
  nautical miles from land and the burial reported to the EPA within 30
  days. Cruise lines that publish a distance use 12 nautical miles.
- Keep the ashes in carry-on luggage when you fly, in a container that
  X-rays clearly; TSA will not open it.
- The ceremony can be cancelled by weather, itinerary changes or local
  rules, so have a backup plan.

## How the law works

### The United States: the EPA general permit

In the United States, burial at sea is governed by the Marine Protection,
Research, and Sanctuaries Act, not the Clean Water Act. The EPA has issued
a general permit, published at
[40 CFR 229.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-229/section-229.1),
that covers cremated remains. According to the
[EPA's Burial at Sea page](https://www.epa.gov/ocean-dumping/burial-sea):

- **No application, prior notice or fee** is needed.
- **Distance:** cremated remains may be placed in ocean waters "of any
  depth" as long as it is "at least three nautical miles from land". The
  EPA measures this from the ordinary low water mark or a closing line
  drawn across the mouths of bays and rivers, not from the beach.
- **Flowers and wreaths** must be readily decomposable. Plastic flowers and
  synthetic wreaths are not allowed, and neither are balloons or other
  devices that are not recovered.
- **Containers:** ashes may go into the sea in a container if it has no
  plastic, does not float and ideally breaks down quickly.
- **Pets:** the permit covers human remains only. Pet ashes cannot be
  mixed with human ashes under it.
- **Reporting:** the burial must be reported within 30 days to the EPA
  Region the vessel departed from, usually through the online
  [Burial at Sea Reporting Tool](https://burialatsea.epa.gov/). The
  [EPA fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/documents/burial_at_sea_reporting_tool_factsheet_may_13_2019.pdf)
  says the person reporting may be a funeral director, a relative or a
  person designated to report, and the EPA page says no death certificate
  needs to be sent.

The 600-foot depth rule you may see online applies to full-body burials,
not to ashes.

### Who the US rule reaches

The general permit covers anyone transporting remains **from the United
States** for burial at sea, and any US-flagged vessel anywhere
([40 CFR 229.1(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-229/section-229.1);
scope in [40 CFR 220.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-220)).
Most large cruise ships fly a foreign flag, but if your ship leaves a US
port with the ashes on board, the US rules apply to that scattering.

Two more points from the EPA page:

- Remains brought from **outside** the United States cannot be placed in US
  ocean waters within 12 nautical miles of shore under the general permit;
  that needs a special permit.
- If remains are taken from the United States into **another country's**
  waters, that country's laws also apply, and "some countries may prohibit
  burials at sea in their waters."

### Territorial waters, international waters, flag and coastal states

Close to land, the coastal country's laws apply. Under the UN Convention on
the Law of the Sea, a country's territorial sea extends up to 12 nautical
miles from its coast (treaty text not re-read for this guide). That is one
reason cruise lines that publish a distance choose 12 nautical miles:
Fred. Olsen says the ship "must be at least 12 nautical miles from land",
and Marella refers to international waters, 12 miles out.

Beyond that, the ship's flag country and the cruise line's own policy
govern what happens on board. A cruise line's permission is a private
policy. It does not override the law of the country whose waters the ship
is in.

### The United Kingdom

GOV.UK says that a marine licence is needed for a burial at sea of a body,
but "you don't need a licence or permission to scatter ashes at sea after a
cremation"
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-get-a-licence-for-a-burial-at-sea-in-england)).
The cruise line's own approval is still required on board.

### Other countries

Rules vary. GOV.UK's guidance for Italy, for example, says residents may be
able to scatter ashes but need authorisation from the town hall
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/when-someone-dies-in-italy)). We have
not yet confirmed the rules for Australia, Canada, Norway, other European
countries, Mexico, the Caribbean or Asia from official sources, so we do
not summarize them here. Ask the cruise line which rules it applies on your
itinerary.

### Protected areas

Some waters are off limits even where the open sea is allowed:

- **US national parks:** scattering ashes in a National Park System area,
  such as Glacier Bay in Alaska, is prohibited except under a permit or in
  designated areas ([36 CFR 2.62(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/chapter-I/part-2/section-2.62)).
- **US marine sanctuaries:** Monterey Bay, Greater Farallones and Channel
  Islands sanctuaries ban discharges from cruise ships except a short list
  of exceptions, and cremated remains are not on that list
  ([15 CFR part 922](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-IX/subchapter-B/part-922)).
- **Cruise line exclusions:** Marella says outside marine protected areas.
  P&O and Cunard reportedly exclude the Norwegian fjords, and Cunard Alaska
  state waters (not yet confirmed).

### Rivers and inland waters

The EPA says scattering ashes in lakes, rivers or other inland waters "is
not subject to federal regulation" under its burial at sea rules. States
may regulate it, and some prohibit it. On a river cruise in Europe, Egypt
or Asia, local rules apply; we found no river cruise line that publishes a
policy, so ask before you book.

## Cruise line policies, one by one

### Royal Caribbean International

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Royal Caribbean's FAQ says: "we allow the tossing of a biodegradable urn
containing ash while at sea." What it requires:

- An approved scattering or biodegradable urn, free of plastic or wire.
- The entire urn goes into the sea, not loose ashes.
- A certificate proving the urn is biodegradable "may also be required."
- The death certificate and the cremation certificate.
- Requests go to gfotravelservices@rccl.com; once on board, contact the
  Front Desk to set a time and place.

Not published: how far ahead to ask, fees, distance, how many can attend,
and whether you receive a certificate or the coordinates. Ask the email
address above.
[Source](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/what-is-royal-caribbeans-burial-at-sea-policy)

### Princess Cruises

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Princess "offers private shipboard ceremonies" for families who want to
rest a loved one's ashes at sea. The ceremony "must be arranged prior to
sailing and approved in advance." Contact ashesrequest@princesscruises.com.

The FAQ does not cover documents, urns, distance, guest numbers or fees.
Ask about each when you write.
[Source](https://www.princess.com/faq/onboard-experience)

### Virgin Voyages

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Virgin answers "Yes" and calls it a Celebration of Life:

- Complete the Celebration of Life Request Form at least 14 days before you
  sail.
- You will be asked for your ship, sail date, party size and a Certificate
  of Biodegradability for the container.
- Virgin checks with local port authorities and the ship, then follows up
  before the voyage.
- Sailor Services can help: 954-488-2955 (US), +44 (20) 30034919 (UK),
  +61 (1800) 491708 (Australia).

Virgin does not offer single-use items such as balloons or individual
flower arrangements on board. Fees, distance and ceremony format are not
published.
[Source](https://www.virginvoyages.com/faq/the-days-at-sea/onboard-policies)

### Fred. Olsen Cruise Lines

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Fred. Olsen publishes the most complete policy we found. Guests may bring
cremated remains or a commemorative wreath on board for a memorial
commemoration:

- Contact Guest Services at least seven days before the cruise (0800 0355
  122 or guest.services@fredolsen.co.uk). The ship's team then contacts you
  on board.
- Keep the ashes in a secure, leakproof, sealed container.
- Everything that goes into the sea must be biodegradable. Ribbons,
  imitation flowers, balloons and similar items are prohibited; a small
  amount of wire or non-plastic string may hold a wreath together.
- To release the container too, show a certificate that it is
  biodegradable once on board.
- The remains stay on board until the service and cannot be taken ashore.
- A senior crew member supervises, for privacy and to follow procedure.
- Place, date and time are agreed between you and the ship's management.
- The ship must be at least 12 nautical miles from land.
- Attendance may be limited, and the service "cannot be guaranteed."

Fees and any certificate afterward are not published.
[Source](https://www.fredolsencruises.com/faqs/on-board/can-i-scatter-ashes-at-sea)

### Marella Cruises (TUI)

*Read through a page-reading tool on November 21, 2026; recheck the exact
wording before publication.*

Marella says ashes "can be scattered in international waters" at least 12
nautical miles from land and outside marine protected areas. Contact TUI
with your booking details before you travel, and tell your airline. On
board, the Guest Experience Coordinator arranges the time. Bring a copy of
the death certificate, and carry the ashes in a leak-proof, sealed
container that can pass X-ray. Anything else that goes overboard must be
biodegradable, and the remains cannot be taken ashore.
[Source](https://www.tui.co.uk/destinations/faq/cruises/marella-cruises-miscellaneous/can-i-bring-a-loved-ones-ashes-onboard-a-marella-cruise-ship-to-be-scattered)

Carnival's help article "Scattering of Ashes" reportedly describes a

### MSC Cruises

*Not yet confirmed: MSC's page blocked our checks.*

MSC's FAQ reportedly offers a "Memorial at Sea" for human ashes only, on
cruises of six nights or longer with at least one sea day, at least 12
nautical miles out. It reportedly asks you to notify MSC before sailing,
carry a certified biodegradable urn in hand luggage, and limits
attendance (regional pages reportedly differ on a 10-guest limit). It

Ask MSC: the guest limit for your region, and what to send before sailing.

### P&O Cruises (UK)

*Not yet confirmed: P&O's page blocked our checks.*

P&O's committal page reportedly lists an admin charge of GBP 270, a

before the cruise, a sea day, at least 12 nautical miles, no committals in
the Norwegian fjords, and a certificate with the latitude and longitude.
Two P&O pages reportedly disagree on the minimum cruise length (no

### Cunard

*Not yet confirmed: Cunard's pages blocked our checks.*

Cunard reportedly offers a paid Committal of Ashes Package. Ashes are
committed in a biodegradable container rather than scattered, with the
Captain's approval in advance; bring the cremation certificate. Cunard
reportedly asks for contact at least 90 days before sailing (the
department is named differently in two versions of the page), and the
Captain or a Senior Officer accompanies the family to an outside area.
[Cunard committal page](https://www.cunard.com/en-gb/activity-types/ceremonies/Committal-of-ashes-at-sea)

### Norwegian, Celebrity, Holland America, Disney and Viking

*No official statement found, November 21, 2026.*

We searched each line's website: FAQs, prohibited-items lists, guest
conduct policies and cruise contracts. None mentions ashes or burial at
sea, and Disney's site could not be read. Holland America asks guests to
"refrain from pouring or discarding anything overboard"
([Know Before You Go](https://www.hollandamerica.com/en/us/plan-a-cruise/get-ready-for-your-cruise/faq/know-before-you-go)), which is why you need the line's

Ask in writing, before you book if you can:

- Do you allow cremated remains to be committed to the sea from this ship
  and itinerary?
- Who do I contact, and how far ahead?
- What documents and what kind of urn do you require? Is the whole urn
  released?
- Is there a fee or a guest limit?
- Will I receive the time and coordinates?
- Who reports it to the EPA if we sail from a US port?

### River and expedition cruises

We found no published policy from any river cruise line (AmaWaterways,
Avalon, Uniworld, Viking River and others) or expedition line (Lindblad,
Hurtigruten, Quark, Ponant and others). Rivers fall under national and
local rules, and polar and protected regions often have their own limits.
Treat every one of these as "ask first."

## Traveling with cremated remains

### Airport security (TSA)

TSA allows cremated remains in carry-on bags, with special instructions,
and in checked bags, but notes that "some airlines do not allow cremated
remains in checked bags." The container must X-ray clearly; one the
officer cannot see through will not be allowed, and officers will not open
it even if you ask. TSA suggests a lighter container such as wood or
plastic.
[TSA](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/cremated-remains)

### Airlines

Airline rules differ, so check your airline's own page before you fly.
Many families keep the ashes in carry-on luggage so they never leave their
sight.

### Customs and other countries

If you fly into another country to meet the ship, check that country's
rules. GOV.UK says no permit is needed to bring ashes into the UK, but to
tell the airline in advance and carry the cremation certificate
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/what-to-do-when-someone-dies-abroad)).

### The cruise terminal and ports of call

Bags are screened at the cruise terminal. Fred. Olsen and Marella both say
the remains cannot be taken ashore once on board, so leave the urn in your
cabin or with the ship until the ceremony.

### Containers

- For travel: a sealed, leakproof container that X-rays clearly.
- For the sea: a biodegradable urn with no plastic or wire, plus the
  maker's certificate of biodegradability, since several lines ask for one.
- You may need two containers: one for travel and one for the ceremony.
  Ask your cruise line whether the transfer can happen on board.

## How to plan it, step by step

1. **Check the policy before you book.** Use the table above, then confirm
   with the cruise line in writing. If the line does not publish a policy,
   ask before you pay.
2. **Choose a suitable sailing.** Look for sea days and time well offshore.
   Some lines reportedly exclude short cruises and some waters, such as the
   Norwegian fjords.
3. **Make the request early.** Notice periods we found range from "on
   board" to 7 days, 14 days and reportedly 90 days. Ask for the approval
   in writing.
4. **Gather documents.** Bring copies of the death certificate and the
   cremation certificate, and the urn's certificate of biodegradability.
5. **Buy the right urn.** Biodegradable, with no plastic or wire, and
   suitable for the line's rule on releasing the whole urn.
6. **Plan the journey.** Carry the ashes in carry-on luggage in an
   X-ray-friendly container, and check your airline's rules.
7. **Confirm on board.** Visit the desk named by your line on the first day
   to confirm the time and place.
8. **Plan the ceremony.** Ask who may attend, whether a crew member stays,
   and whether flowers are allowed (they must be biodegradable). Some lines
   sell flowers on board.
9. **If weather or the itinerary prevents it,** ask the ship whether
   another sea day is possible. If not, the ashes stay in your care: keep
   the paperwork, take them home, and consider a charter boat scattering
   service or, for eligible veterans, the US Navy burial at sea program.
   Do not scatter them yourself.
10. **Afterward,** ask for the date, time and coordinates. If you sailed
    from a US port, ask the cruise line whether it files the EPA report;
    if not, you can file it yourself through the EPA's online tool within
    30 days.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I scatter ashes from my cruise ship balcony?

No. Every cruise line policy we read requires the line's permission and a
ceremony arranged by the ship. Throwing anything overboard without staff
permission can lead to a charge: Princess's passage contract and Holland
America's cruise contract each allow a US $500 charge per violation
([Princess](https://www.princess.com/legal/passage-contract/plc),
[Holland America](https://www.hollandamerica.com/en/us/legal-privacy/cruise-contract)).

### Is the urn released, or are the ashes poured out?

It depends on the line. Royal Caribbean says the entire urn must be tossed.
Fred. Olsen allows either, if the container is certified biodegradable.
Wind can blow loose ashes back on board, which is one reason many lines
prefer the whole urn.

### Do cruise lines charge for this?

Some do. P&O reportedly charges GBP 270 and Cunard sells a package; MSC
reportedly charges nothing. Most lines we read do not publish a fee, so ask.

### How far from shore does the ship have to be?

US rules require at least 3 nautical miles. Cruise lines that publish a
distance use 12 nautical miles.

### Do I have to tell the EPA?

If the ship departed a US port with the ashes on board, yes: the burial
must be reported within 30 days. Ask the cruise line whether it reports
it. If not, anyone responsible for the arrangements, including a relative,
can report online.

### Can I choose where the ashes go?

Usually the ship decides with you. Fred. Olsen agrees the place, date and
time with guests; Royal Caribbean's Front Desk sets the time and place.

### Will I receive the coordinates or a certificate?

None of the policies we read directly promise one. P&O and MSC reportedly
provide a certificate with the position (not yet confirmed). Ask in
advance.

### Can I scatter a pet's ashes at sea from a cruise?

Not under the US general permit, which covers human remains only, and MSC
reportedly excludes animal ashes. Ask the line.

### Can I scatter ashes at a private island or in port?

Not that we found. Fred. Olsen and Marella say the remains cannot be taken
ashore once on board.

### What if the ceremony can't happen?

Fred. Olsen states the service "cannot be guaranteed," and other lines
depend on port authorities, weather and the itinerary. Keep the ashes and
paperwork safe and plan another option.

### Can I keep some of the ashes?

Cruise policies we read do not address this. Ask the line whether a
partial amount can be committed.
