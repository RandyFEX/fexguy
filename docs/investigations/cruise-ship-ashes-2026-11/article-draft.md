# Scattering Ashes From a Cruise Ship: Cruise Line Policies, Rules and Planning Guide

## Quick answer

Some cruise lines will help you commit a loved one's ashes to the sea, but
only with their permission and on their terms. Royal Caribbean, Princess,
Virgin Voyages, Fred. Olsen and Marella publish a written process.
Ambassador says it may be possible case by case, and Crystal says it does
not offer burials at sea from the ship. Most other lines, including
Norwegian, Celebrity, Holland America, Disney and Viking, publish no policy
we could find, and the pages of Carnival, MSC, P&O Cruises and Cunard could
not be read for this guide. For any line, ask in writing before you book.
Plan on a biodegradable urn, copies of the death and cremation
certificates, a request made before you sail, and a ceremony well
offshore, often 12 nautical miles out.

Key points:

- Never scatter ashes from your balcony or a public deck on your own. Ask
  the cruise line first; the ship arranges the time and place.
- Published policies require a biodegradable container, and several
  require the whole urn to go into the sea rather than loose ashes.
- A cruise line's permission does not override the law of the country
  whose waters the ship is in. Close to shore, local rules apply.
- From a US port, cremated remains must be placed at least 3 nautical
  miles from land and the burial reported to the EPA within 30 days.
- Airport rules differ by country and airline. Air Canada, for example, no
  longer accepts cremated remains in checked bags from August 28, 2026.

## How the law works

Three layers apply at once: the law of the country whose waters the ship
is in, the law of the ship's flag country, and the cruise line's own
policy. A cruise line's permission is a private rule. It never overrides
the law of the place.

### International waters, territorial waters and the flag state

Under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, a country's sovereignty
covers its territorial sea, which may extend up to 12 nautical miles from
its baselines (Articles 2 and 3), and it can enforce some laws in a
contiguous zone up to 24 nautical miles (Article 33). On the high seas, a
ship is subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of its flag country (Article
92). Dumping in another country's territorial sea or exclusive economic
zone needs that country's express prior approval (Article 210(5))
([UNCLOS text](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/unclos_e.pdf)).

The treaty does not say whether scattering ashes counts as "dumping", and
neither does the IMO's page on the London Convention and Protocol
([IMO](https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/Environment/Pages/London-Convention-Protocol.aspx)).
The 12-mile limit helps explain why cruise lines that publish a distance
choose 12 nautical miles: Fred. Olsen says the ship "must be at least 12
nautical miles from land", and Marella refers to international waters.

### The United States: the EPA general permit

In the United States, burial at sea is governed by the Marine Protection,
Research, and Sanctuaries Act, not the Clean Water Act. The EPA's general
permit, published at
[40 CFR 229.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-229/section-229.1),
covers cremated remains. According to the
[EPA's Burial at Sea page](https://www.epa.gov/ocean-dumping/burial-sea):

- **No application, prior notice or fee** is needed.
- **Distance:** cremated remains may be placed in ocean waters "of any
  depth" as long as it is "at least three nautical miles from land",
  measured from the low water mark or a closing line across bays and
  rivers, not from the beach.
- **Flowers and wreaths** must be readily decomposable. No plastic
  flowers, synthetic wreaths or balloons.
- **Containers:** ashes may go into the sea in a container that has no
  plastic, does not float and ideally breaks down quickly.
- **Pets:** the permit covers human remains only, and pet ashes cannot be
  mixed with human ashes under it.
- **Reporting:** the burial must be reported within 30 days to the EPA
  Region the vessel departed from, usually through the online
  [Burial at Sea Reporting Tool](https://burialatsea.epa.gov/). The
  [EPA fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/documents/burial_at_sea_reporting_tool_factsheet_may_13_2019.pdf)
  says the person reporting may be a funeral director, a relative or a
  person designated to report.

The rule covers anyone taking remains **from the United States** for
burial at sea and any US-flagged vessel anywhere. Most cruise ships fly a
foreign flag, but if your ship leaves a US port with the ashes aboard, the
US rules apply. Remains brought from outside the United States cannot be
placed in US ocean waters within 12 nautical miles under the general
permit, and in another country's waters that country's laws also apply;
"some countries may prohibit burials at sea in their waters" (EPA). The
600-foot depth rule you may see online is for full-body burials, not
ashes.

Alaska and Hawaii: we found no state rule on scattering ashes at sea. In
areas of the National Park System, scattering ashes is prohibited except
under a permit or in designated areas
([36 CFR 2.62](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/chapter-I/part-2/section-2.62)),
and the Monterey Bay, Greater Farallones and Channel Islands marine
sanctuaries ban discharges from cruise ships with exceptions that do not
include ashes
([15 CFR part 922](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-IX/subchapter-B/part-922)).

### United Kingdom and Ireland

- **England and Northern Irish offshore waters:** "You don't need a
  licence or permission to scatter ashes at sea after a cremation"
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-get-a-licence-for-a-burial-at-sea-in-england)).
- **Scotland:** Scottish Government guidance says "an application for
  burial is not required for the scattering of ashes"
  ([gov.scot](https://www.gov.scot/publications/burial-regulations-guidance/pages/3/)).
- **Wales:** we found no statement from Natural Resources Wales.
- **Ireland:** the Department of Transport says burial at sea is
  permitted and its voluntary guidelines suggest cremation and scattering
  of ashes be considered instead
  ([gov.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-transport/services/burial-at-sea/)).

### Europe and the Mediterranean

Close to shore, national rules can be strict. What we confirmed from
official texts:

- **Norway:** scattering ashes needs the County Governor's permission
  ([Gravferdsloven § 20, Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1996-06-07-32)).
  On **Svalbard**, the Governor may permit it on application, not in
  built-up areas
  ([Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2008-02-15-153)).
- **Netherlands:** relatives may scatter ashes in state waters, at sea or
  in a river, without a permit, keeping a distance from bathing sites and
  drinking-water intakes
  ([Rijkswaterstaat](https://www.rijkswaterstaat.nl/water/wetten-regels-en-vergunningen/vergunningen-rijkswateren)).
- **Italy:** dispersal at sea, in lakes and rivers is allowed only "in
  stretches free of boats and structures" and needs the civil-status
  officer's authorization
  ([Law 130/2001](https://www.parlamento.it/parlam/leggi/01130l.htm)).
  Local Coast Guard ordinances add distances; one district, for example,
  bans dispersal within 1,000 meters of the coast
  ([Ordinance 97/2023](https://www.guardiacostiera.gov.it/portale/documents/121759/0/Ord+97+del+07-09-2023+Dispersione+delle+ceneri+funebri+in+mare.pdf/df0e6413-6c14-1a90-20a3-6d45de39a849?version=1.0&t=1742803425272)).
- **Spain:** businesses that place urns or ashes at sea must file a
  declaration with the Maritime Captaincy, use biodegradable urns and log
  coordinates ([Royal Decree 218/2022](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2022/03/29/218)).
- **France:** the Ministry of Justice's guide says dispersal or immersion
  of a biodegradable urn "est autorisée en pleine mer" (is allowed at
  sea), with a declaration to the town hall of the deceased's birthplace
  ([justice.fr](https://www.justice.fr/fiche/cremation)). We could not
  read the underlying code on Legifrance.
- **Croatia:** scattering only at places designated by the local council
  ([Cemeteries Act 2025](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2025_05_78_1014.html)).
- **Germany:** a sea burial is the lowering of a water-soluble urn on the
  high seas, with permits
  ([Karlsruhe service page](https://web1.karlsruhe.de/service/Buergerdienste/leistung.php?id=6015473)).
- **Greece:** we could not confirm the rule from an official text.

### Canada, Australia and New Zealand

- **Canada:** the federal disposal-at-sea law (CEPA 1999, section 125)
  does not mention ashes
  ([Justice Laws](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-15.31/FullText.html)).
  We could not open the federal guidance pages.
- **Australia:** the Sea Dumping Act does not mention ashes
  ([legislation.gov.au](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02478/latest/text));
  the government's guidance on ashes could not be opened, and we found no
  Great Barrier Reef Marine Park rule on ashes.
- **New Zealand:** not confirmed from an official page.

### Caribbean, Bahamas, Mexico and Bermuda

We found no official rule on scattering ashes for the Bahamas, Mexico,
Bermuda or most Caribbean states. Ask the cruise line which local rules
apply on your itinerary.

### Asia

- **Japan:** the Ministry of Health's guideline for scattering businesses
  asks for scattering at sea a suitable distance from the coast, without
  setting a number ([MHLW](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11130500/001321304.pdf)).
- **Singapore:** sea scattering takes place at a site 3.2 km east of
  Raffles Lighthouse
  ([NEA](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/after-death/post-death-matters/burial-cremation-and-ash-storage)).
- **Hong Kong:** three designated areas; the FEHB 198 form must be
  approved first ([Green Burial](https://www.greenburial.gov.hk/en/scattering-cremains-sea/intro.html)).

### Polar regions and the Galapagos

- **Antarctica:** the Antarctic Treaty's environmental protocol limits
  what ships may discharge but does not mention human remains
  ([Annex IV](https://documents.ats.aq/recatt/Att011_e.pdf)). We found no
  rule that allows or bans ashes, so expedition lines must be asked.
- **South Georgia:** the government permits scattering on the island,
  with 60 days' written notice; not on the South Sandwich Islands
  ([GSGSSI policy](https://gov.gs/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Scattering-of-Ashes-Policy-0321.pdf)).
- **Svalbard:** see Norway above.
- **Galapagos:** no National Park rule found.

### Rivers and inland waters

The EPA says scattering in lakes and rivers "is not subject to federal
regulation" under its burial at sea rules; states may regulate it. In
Europe, rules vary: the Netherlands allows it in state rivers without a
permit, Italy only with authorization, and Rhineland-Palatinate in
Germany allows a water-soluble urn on the Rhine, Moselle, Lahn or Saar
only for its own residents
([BestattG RP](https://mwg.rlp.de/fileadmin/15/Abteilung_2_Gesundheit/Bestattungsgesetz/BestattG_RP_2025.pdf)).
We found no river cruise line that publishes a policy.

## Cruise line policies, one by one

### Royal Caribbean International

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Royal Caribbean's FAQ says: "we allow the tossing of a biodegradable urn
containing ash while at sea." What it requires:

- An approved scattering or biodegradable urn, free of plastic or wire.
- The entire urn goes into the sea, not loose ashes.
- A certificate proving the urn is biodegradable "may also be required."
- The death certificate and the cremation certificate.
- Requests go to gfotravelservices@rccl.com; once on board, contact the
  Front Desk to set a time and place.

Not published: how far ahead to ask, fees, distance, how many can attend,

### Princess Cruises

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Princess "offers private shipboard ceremonies" for families who want to
rest a loved one's ashes at sea. The ceremony "must be arranged prior to
sailing and approved in advance." Contact ashesrequest@princesscruises.com.
Documents, urns, distance, guest numbers and fees are not published.
[Source](https://www.princess.com/faq/onboard-experience)

### Virgin Voyages

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Virgin answers "Yes" and calls it a Celebration of Life:

- Complete the Celebration of Life Request Form at least 14 days before you
  sail.
- You will be asked for your ship, sail date, party size and a Certificate
  of Biodegradability for the container.
- Virgin checks with local port authorities and the ship, then follows up
  before the voyage.
- Sailor Services: 954-488-2955 (US), +44 (20) 30034919 (UK),
  +61 (1800) 491708 (Australia).

Virgin does not offer single-use items such as balloons or individual

### Fred. Olsen Cruise Lines

*Read on the official page, November 21, 2026.*

Guests may bring cremated remains or a commemorative wreath on board for a
memorial commemoration:

- Contact Guest Services at least seven days before the cruise (0800 0355
  122 or guest.services@fredolsen.co.uk).
- Keep the ashes in a secure, leakproof, sealed container.
- Everything that goes into the sea must be biodegradable; ribbons,
  imitation flowers and balloons are prohibited.
- To release the container too, show a certificate that it is
  biodegradable once on board.
- The remains cannot be taken ashore once on board.
- A senior crew member supervises; place, date and time are agreed with
  the ship's management.
- The ship must be at least 12 nautical miles from land.
- Attendance may be limited, and the service "cannot be guaranteed."

[Source](https://www.fredolsencruises.com/faqs/on-board/can-i-scatter-ashes-at-sea)

### Lines with no published policy

We searched the official websites (FAQs, guest contracts, terms,
prohibited-items lists and sitemaps) of every other operator in our

- **Large ocean lines:** Norwegian, Celebrity, Holland America, Disney,
  Viking, Costa, AIDA, TUI Cruises (Mein Schiff), Celestyal.
- **Premium and luxury:** Oceania, Regent Seven Seas, Seabourn, Silversea,
  Azamara, Explora Journeys, Windstar, SeaDream, Ritz-Carlton Yacht

- **Coastal and expedition:** Hurtigruten, Havila, HX, Ponant,
  Hapag-Lloyd, Lindblad, Quark, Aurora, Scenic, Emerald, Atlas, American

- **River:** AmaWaterways, Avalon, Uniworld, Tauck, Riviera, CroisiEurope,
  A-ROSA, nicko, APT, Scenic and Emerald river, Grand Circle and others.

Some lines' contracts and onboard rules ban throwing items overboard
without the crew's permission. Holland America asks guests to "refrain
from pouring or discarding anything overboard"
([Know Before You Go](https://www.hollandamerica.com/en/us/plan-a-cruise/get-ready-for-your-cruise/faq/know-before-you-go)),
and the passage contracts of Princess and Holland America allow a US $500
charge per violation. That is a general rule, not a ban on ceremonies,
but it is why you need written permission first.

Ask any of these lines, in writing, before you book:

- Do you allow cremated remains to be committed to the sea from this ship
  and itinerary?
- Who do I contact, and how far ahead?
- What documents and what kind of urn do you require? Is the whole urn
  released?
- Is there a fee or a guest limit?
- Will I receive the time and coordinates?
- Who reports it to the EPA if we sail from a US port?

## Traveling with cremated remains

Airport rules are national, and airlines add their own. Do not assume US
rules apply abroad.

### United States (TSA)

TSA allows cremated remains in carry-on bags, with special instructions,
and in checked bags, but says some airlines do not allow them in checked
bags. The container must X-ray clearly. "Out of respect for the deceased,
TSA officers will not open a container, even if requested by the
passenger," and the officer has the final say. TSA suggests a lighter
container such as wood or plastic
([TSA](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/cremated-remains);
read through a page-reading tool).

### Canada

CATSA says screening officers are not permitted to open a cremation
container, and that "smaller amounts of cremated remains (less than 350
ml) are more likely to be successfully screened" ([CATSA](https://www.catsa-acsta.gc.ca/en/cremated-remains)).

### Australia and New Zealand

- Australian Border Force: "There are no requirements for importing human
  ashes into Australia," but there are rules for the container
  ([ABF](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing/how-to-import/types-of-imports/importing-human-remains-ashes)).
- New Zealand Aviation Security: a carry-on container "will be inspected,
  but not opened"
  ([Aviation Security](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/passenger-information/what-can-i-bring/show/ashes-cremated-remains/)),
  and NZ Customs needs no clearance for ashes you carry in
  ([NZ Customs](https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/prohibited-and-restricted-items)).

### United Kingdom

GOV.UK says no permit is needed to bring ashes into the UK, but to tell
the airline in advance and carry the cremation certificate
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/what-to-do-when-someone-dies-abroad)).
Heathrow exempts human ashes from its 350 g powder limit on US flights,
but they may need extra screening
([Heathrow](https://www.heathrow.com/at-the-airport/security-and-baggage/hand-baggage-and-liquids)).

check with your airline.

### Customs into the United States

CDC's import rules for human remains do not apply to remains already
fully cremated before import
([42 CFR 71.50](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-71/subpart-F/section-71.50)).

### The cruise terminal and ports of call

US cruise terminals may X-ray baggage under an approved security program
([33 CFR 105.545](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-105/subpart-E/section-105.545)).
Fred. Olsen and Marella both say the remains cannot be taken ashore once
on board, so leave the urn with the ship until the ceremony.

### Containers

- For travel: a sealed, leakproof container that X-rays clearly.
- For the sea: a biodegradable urn with no plastic or wire, plus the
  maker's certificate of biodegradability, since several lines ask for one.
- You may need two containers. Ask your cruise line whether the transfer
  can happen on board.

## How to plan it, step by step

1. **Check the policy before you book.** Use the table above, then confirm
   with the cruise line in writing. If the line publishes no policy, ask
   before you pay.
2. **Choose a suitable sailing.** Look for sea days and time well
   offshore. Some lines reportedly exclude short cruises and some waters,
   such as the Norwegian fjords, and many coastal countries have their
   own rules close to shore.
3. **Make the request early.** Notice periods we found range from "on
   board" to 7 and 14 days. Ask for the approval in writing.
4. **Gather documents.** Copies of the death certificate and cremation
   certificate, and the urn's certificate of biodegradability.
5. **Buy the right urn.** Biodegradable, no plastic or wire, suitable for
   releasing whole.
6. **Plan the journey.** Check your airline's own rules and the rules of
   each airport you pass through.
7. **Confirm on board.** On the first day, visit the desk your line names
   to confirm the time and place.
8. **Plan the ceremony.** Ask who may attend, whether a crew member stays,
   and whether flowers are allowed (biodegradable only).
9. **If weather or the itinerary prevents it,** ask whether another sea
   day is possible. If not, keep the ashes and paperwork, take them home,
   and consider a licensed charter boat or, for eligible veterans, the US
   Navy burial at sea program. Do not scatter them yourself.
10. **Afterward,** ask for the date, time and coordinates. If you sailed
    from a US port, ask whether the cruise line files the EPA report; if
    not, you can report online within 30 days.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I scatter ashes from my cruise ship balcony?

No. Every policy we read requires the line's permission and a ceremony
arranged by the ship, and some contracts allow a US $500 charge for
releasing items overboard without permission
([Princess](https://www.princess.com/legal/passage-contract/plc),
[Holland America](https://www.hollandamerica.com/en/us/legal-privacy/cruise-contract)).

### Is the urn released, or are the ashes poured out?

It depends on the line. Royal Caribbean says the entire urn must be tossed.
Fred. Olsen allows either, if the container is certified biodegradable.

### Do cruise lines charge for this?

None of the policies we read publishes a fee. Ask.

### How far from shore does the ship have to be?

From a US port, at least 3 nautical miles by law. Lines that publish a
distance use 12 nautical miles. Other countries set their own rules.

### Do I have to tell the EPA?

If the ship departed a US port with the ashes aboard, the burial must be
reported within 30 days. Ask the cruise line whether it reports it; if
not, a relative can report online.

### Can I choose where the ashes go?

Usually the ship decides with you. Fred. Olsen agrees the place, date and
time with guests; Royal Caribbean's Front Desk sets the time and place.

### Will I receive the coordinates or a certificate?

None of the policies we read promises one. Ask in advance.

### Can I scatter a pet's ashes at sea from a cruise?

Not under the US general permit, which covers human remains only. Ask the
line.

### Can I scatter ashes at a private island or in port?

Not that we found. Fred. Olsen and Marella say the remains cannot be taken
ashore once on board, and coastal countries set their own rules.

### What if the ceremony can't happen?

Fred. Olsen states the service "cannot be guaranteed," and other lines
depend on port authorities, weather and the itinerary. Keep the ashes and
paperwork safe and plan another option.

### Can I keep some of the ashes?

The policies we read do not address this. Ask the line.
