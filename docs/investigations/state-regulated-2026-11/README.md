### Episode 2: The Investigation (about 75 s)

"I wanted to know what regulators have actually said about these mailers. So
I looked.
[Graphic: D.C. 2020]
In 2020, Washington D.C.'s insurance department warned about a mailer headed
'F-1 2020 Benefit Information for District of Columbia Residents Only.' They
said the information people send back may be used by, or sold to, insurance
companies.
[Graphic: Iowa 2020]
The same year, Iowa's Insurance Division ordered a mailer operation to cease
and desist. One of its mailers used the words 'state-regulated program.'
The company was paid for each lead. It later signed a consent judgment with
the Iowa Attorney General for $40,000, without admitting a violation.
[Graphic: Nebraska 2022]
In 2022, Nebraska's insurance department showed a 'T-2' card headed '2020
Benefit Information for Nebraska Citizens Only.'
[Randy on camera]
Different letters, different years, same playbook. The card in my hand
isn't, by itself, proof anyone broke the law. But now you know what this
style of card is for: getting your name to someone who sells insurance."

### Episode 3: The Big Check (about 70 s)

"These cards promise a benefit 'up to $25,000' that's 'tax-free for state
residents.'
[Graphic: IRS Publication 525]
Life insurance paid to your beneficiary because of a death generally isn't
taxable income, in every state. That's not a special state benefit.
[Randy on camera]
What matters is which kind of policy you end up with. Some policies pay the
full amount from the first day. Guaranteed acceptance policies don't ask
health questions, and they have a two-year waiting period for natural death.
[Graphic: Gerber Guaranteed Life, 65-year-old woman, $10,000, Texas rates]
Take a 65-year-old woman with $10,000 of Gerber Guaranteed Life. Her premium
is $62.61 a month. If she dies of natural causes at month 18, her family gets
about $1,240, the premiums plus 10%, not $10,000. After two years, the full
$10,000.
If you're healthy enough to qualify for first-day coverage, that waiting
period is a cost you don't have to carry."

## 4. Pricing sources and method

- Mutual of Omaha Living Promise Level (FF-102), Aetna Accendo Preferred
  (FF-103) and Gerber Guaranteed Life ACH (FF-201): the site's rate engine
  (server/rates/), from the published tables (rates:v1:production, published
  2026-11-06T00:05:03Z; preview tables match). Texas reference state.
- Colonial Penn Guaranteed Acceptance: src/data/colonial-penn-units.json
  (coverage per unit by age 50 to 80 and sex, $9.95 a unit, 25-unit maximum).
  Coverage is the fewest whole units at or above the amount chosen; the other
  plans are re-priced at that exact coverage for the matched comparison.
- Differences: monthly difference x 12 x years; level premiums, no interest,
  policy in force. Meals = total / meal cost ($10 default); calories = meals x
  667; food days = calories / 2,000. Income share = monthly difference /
  entered monthly income (never stored or sent anywhere but the calculator's
  own page).
- Benefits: level plans pay the face amount from day one; Gerber pays
  premiums plus 10% for natural death in the first two years; Colonial Penn's
  first-two-year natural-death benefit is shown only as "at least the
  premiums paid" until its exact contract terms are confirmed.
- Endpoint: POST /api/impact-calculator/ returns calculated premiums only,
  with opaque codes (no product ids, rates or tables); same-origin, 256-byte
  bodies, per-IP limits 60 per 10 minutes and 300 a day.

Verified examples (65-year-old woman, non-tobacco, Texas):

| Plan | Coverage | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Colonial Penn Guaranteed Acceptance (8 units) | $10,064 | $79.60 |
| Gerber Guaranteed Life | $10,000 / $10,064 | $62.61 / $63.00 |
| Aetna Accendo Preferred | $10,000 / $10,064 | $47.60 / $47.88 |
| Mutual of Omaha Living Promise Level | $10,000 / $10,064 | $41.01 / $41.25 |

Age 80, $10,000: Gerber $176.37 (female) / $247.32 (male); Mutual of Omaha
Level $98.43 / $139.73. Colonial Penn coverage per unit falls from $1,761
(age 55 female) to $608 (age 80 female).
